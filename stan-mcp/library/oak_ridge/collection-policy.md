# Oak Ridge Estates Collection Policy
<!-- type: collection; adopted: 2012-01-01; amended: 2022-01-01 -->

This Collection Policy explains how and when the annual assessment is billed, what happens when a payment is late, and how the Association pursues an account that falls seriously behind, including the lien procedure described in the Declaration. The Board adopted this Policy January 1, 2012 and amended it January 1, 2022 to formalize the quarterly payment plan option.

## Article 1 - Billing and Due Dates

### 1.1 Annual Assessment Billing

The Association bills the annual assessment of $1,450.00 per Lot each year, due in full on January 31, with notice of the amount sent by mail or email at least 30 days before the due date once the Board adopts the coming year's budget. An Owner who pays in full by January 31 avoids the late fee and interest described in Article 2 entirely. The administrator posts each Owner's account balance and payment history on request during posted office hours.

### 1.2 Payment Plans

An Owner may request a quarterly payment plan, paying $362.50 on February 1, May 1, August 1 and November 1 instead of the full $1,450.00 on January 31, by notifying the administrator before the January 31 due date. An Owner already on a quarterly plan who misses a quarterly payment is treated the same as a missed annual payment for purposes of the late fee and the six-month lien threshold in Article 3, counted from the missed installment's due date rather than from January 31.

## Article 2 - Late Payments

### 2.1 Late Fee

A $75.00 late fee is added to an Owner's account for any assessment balance still unpaid after March 1, regardless of whether the Owner is on the standard annual schedule or a quarterly payment plan. The late fee is a one-time charge per year's assessment, not a repeating monthly charge, though interest continues to accrue separately under Section 2.2 on any balance that remains unpaid after the late fee is added.

### 2.2 Interest on Unpaid Balances

Interest accrues on any assessment, late fee, or fine that remains unpaid 30 days after it becomes due, at a rate of 6 percent per year, calculated monthly on the outstanding balance. Interest stops accruing on the portion of a balance that is paid, and the administrator applies any partial payment first to the oldest outstanding interest, then to fees, then to the assessment principal, unless the Board agrees to a different order as part of a payment plan.

## Article 3 - Collections and Liens

### 3.1 Demand Letters and Account Review

When an account is more than 60 days past due, the administrator sends a written demand letter summarizing the balance, the late fee and any accrued interest, and reminding the Owner of the quarterly payment plan option under Section 1.2. The Board reviews any account still unresolved after the demand letter at its next regular meeting and decides whether to pursue a lien under Section 3.2 or to work out a payment arrangement under Article 4.

### 3.2 Filing a Lien

If an account remains delinquent for six months from its original due date, the Association may record a lien against the Lot for the full unpaid balance, including assessments, late fees, interest, any related fines, and the Association's reasonable collection costs and attorney's fees, as provided in the Declaration. The Board authorizes each lien filing individually rather than filing automatically at the six-month mark, giving the administrator a chance to confirm a payment is not already in process.

### 3.3 Release of Lien and Payment in Full

Once an Owner pays the full balance secured by a lien, including any collection costs the Association incurred in filing it, the Association records a release of the lien within a reasonable time, typically within 30 days. A partial payment does not entitle an Owner to a partial release; the lien remains on the Lot until the full secured balance is paid or the Board agrees to a different resolution.

## Article 4 - Hardship and Payment Arrangements

### 4.1 Requesting a Payment Plan

An Owner who cannot pay the full annual assessment by January 31 should contact the administrator as soon as possible to request the standard quarterly plan under Section 1.2, or, in a hardship such as illness or job loss, a different schedule the Board may approve. Requesting a plan before a payment is missed avoids the late fee and keeps the account out of the collection process in Article 3.

### 4.2 Board Discretion

The Board may approve a payment arrangement outside the standard quarterly plan, such as a longer schedule or a temporary reduction in required payments, where an Owner shows genuine hardship, but is not required to waive the late fee, interest, or a fine already assessed except in unusual circumstances the Board documents in its minutes. Any approved arrangement is in writing and signed by the Owner and an officer of the Association.
