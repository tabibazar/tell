# Bylaws of Oak Ridge Estates Property Owners Association
<!-- type: bylaws; adopted: 1985-03-15 -->

These Bylaws govern the internal affairs of the Oak Ridge Estates Property Owners Association, a nonprofit association of the Owners of the 58 Lots in Oak Ridge Estates, adopted March 15, 1985 alongside the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions. Where these Bylaws address membership, meetings, the Board of Directors, officers, committees and records, the Declaration controls on matters of land use and assessments.

## Article 1 - Membership and Voting

### 1.1 Membership

Every record Owner of a Lot in Oak Ridge Estates is automatically a member of the Association for as long as the Owner holds title, and membership ends when the Lot is sold or transferred. Membership is not transferable apart from the Lot and carries no certificate or separate fee beyond the annual assessment set under the Declaration. Where a Lot is owned by more than one person, the co-owners hold a single membership and must designate, in writing filed with the administrator, which of them casts the Lot's vote, or else their vote will not be counted if they disagree at a meeting.

### 1.2 Voting Rights

Each Lot carries exactly one vote in Association matters, regardless of its acreage or the number of people residing on it, so the 58 Lots in Oak Ridge Estates carry 58 total votes. The vote for a Lot is cast by its Owner or designated co-owner, or by the Owner's written proxy under Section 1.4. Votes are counted per Lot, not per person, at the annual meeting, any special meeting, and any written ballot the Board circulates between meetings for a special assessment or amendment vote.

### 1.3 Quorum

A quorum for any meeting of the Owners is 30 percent of the Lots, currently 18 of the 58 Lots, present in person or represented by proxy. If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes of the announced start time, the President may adjourn the meeting to a date within the next 30 days, at which the same 30 percent quorum applies; the Board will give at least 10 days' notice of the reconvened meeting. Business requiring a specific vote threshold higher than a simple majority, such as a Declaration amendment, still requires that higher threshold regardless of how many Lots are present.

### 1.4 Proxies

An Owner unable to attend a meeting may appoint any adult, including another Owner, as proxy to cast that Lot's vote, by a signed proxy form filed with the administrator or presented at the meeting's registration table before voting begins. A proxy is valid only for the specific meeting named on it, or any adjournment of that meeting, and may be revoked at any time before the vote by the Owner's written notice or by the Owner's own appearance at the meeting. The Board's standard proxy form is available from the administrator at the barn office or posted on the Association's website before each meeting.

## Article 2 - Meetings of the Owners

### 2.1 Annual Meeting

The Association holds its annual meeting each spring; the next annual meeting is scheduled for April 2027 at the community barn. At the annual meeting, the Owners elect directors to fill any Board seats whose terms have ended, hear the Treasurer's report on the past year and the budget for the coming year, and act on any amendment or special assessment properly noticed for that meeting. The Board sets the exact date, time and location at least 60 days in advance and notifies all Owners by mail or email at least 30 days before the meeting.

### 2.2 Special Meetings

The Board may call a special meeting of the Owners at any time to address a specific matter, such as a special assessment or an urgent amendment, and must call one within 30 days of receiving a written petition signed by Owners holding at least 20 percent of the Lots stating the purpose of the requested meeting. Notice of a special meeting must state its purpose, and only that purpose may be voted on at the meeting. The Board's regular meeting on November 14, 2026, for example, is a Board meeting open to Owner attendance rather than a special meeting of the Owners, since it does not require an Owner vote.

### 2.3 Notice of Meetings

The Board gives Owners written notice of every annual or special meeting at least 10 days before the meeting date, by mail, email, or posting at the cluster mailboxes and on the Association's website, stating the date, time, place and, for a special meeting, its purpose. Notice of a regular Board meeting, which any Owner may attend and speak at, is posted at least 7 days in advance on the website and at the barn office. Minutes of past meetings are posted on the Association's website once approved, typically at the next meeting.

### 2.4 Order of Business and Homeowner Forum

Meetings generally follow the order of call to order and roll call, approval of prior minutes, the Treasurer's report, committee reports, old business, new business, a homeowner forum, and adjournment. During the homeowner forum, any Owner present may speak on any Association matter, and the President keeps time to allow all who wish to speak a fair turn, typically limiting each speaker to a few minutes when many Owners wish to be heard. The Board is not required to vote on a matter raised only in the forum but will note it in the minutes and may take it up as new business at a future meeting.

## Article 3 - Board of Directors

### 3.1 Number, Qualifications and Terms

The Board consists of three directors, each an Owner or a co-owner of a Lot, elected at large by the Owners to staggered two-year terms so that the Board does not turn over entirely in a single year. The current Board is Walt Harmon, President, whose term ends in 2026, Debbie Sloane, Treasurer, whose term ends in 2027, and Frank Ito, who chairs the Roads Committee as a director and whose term also ends in 2027. A director whose term is ending may stand for re-election at the annual meeting with no limit on the number of terms served.

### 3.2 Powers and Duties of the Board

The Board manages the business and affairs of the Association between meetings of the Owners, including adopting the annual budget, setting the assessment, hiring and overseeing the administrator and contractors such as Hollis Plowing and Turner Excavating, enforcing this Declaration and the Rules and Regulations, and deciding architectural review appeals. The Board acts by majority vote of the directors present at a properly noticed Board meeting, and a director may not vote by proxy, though a director unable to attend may submit written comments to be read into the record. No single director may bind the Association to a contract or expenditure outside the adopted budget without a Board vote.

### 3.3 Board Meetings and Quorum

The Board meets regularly, roughly every six to eight weeks, at the community barn, with the meeting open to any Owner who wishes to attend and speak during the homeowner forum. A quorum for Board action is a majority of the three directors, meaning at least two directors must be present to conduct business. The Board may also act without a meeting by unanimous written consent signed by all three directors, which the Secretary files with the meeting minutes. Emergency road or dam matters, such as a washed-out culvert, may be handled by the Roads chair acting under standing authority the Board has already granted, with a report to the full Board at its next meeting.

### 3.4 Vacancies and Removal

If a director resigns, moves away, or otherwise cannot finish a term, the remaining directors appoint a replacement Owner to serve until the next annual meeting, at which the Owners elect someone to fill any remaining portion of the term. An Owner may petition for a director's removal before the end of a term by presenting a petition signed by at least 25 percent of the Lots to a special meeting called under Section 2.2, where removal requires a majority vote of the Lots represented at that meeting. A director who no longer owns a Lot in Oak Ridge Estates automatically vacates the Board seat.

## Article 4 - Officers

### 4.1 Officers Generally

The Board selects a President and a Treasurer from among its three directors at the first Board meeting after each annual meeting; because the Board is small, the Treasurer also keeps the Association's minutes and correspondence in the Secretary's stead unless the Board designates the role separately. Officers serve at the Board's pleasure and may be replaced by majority vote of the directors at any time. An officer's duties under this Article are in addition to that person's duties simply as a director under Article 3.

### 4.2 President

The President presides at all meetings of the Board and of the Owners, keeps time during the homeowner forum so each speaker gets a fair turn, signs contracts and recorded documents on the Association's behalf once the Board has approved them, and serves as the Association's primary point of contact with Hollis County and outside agencies. Walt Harmon currently serves as President, with a term ending in 2026; the Board will select a President, who may or may not be the same director, at its first meeting after the 2027 annual election.

### 4.3 Treasurer and Secretary

The Treasurer keeps the Association's financial books, collects assessments with the administrator's help, prepares the annual budget for the Board's adoption, and reports on the Association's finances, including the reserve fund balance, at every Board meeting and the annual meeting. Acting also as Secretary, the Treasurer keeps or oversees the minutes of Board and Owner meetings, maintains the Association's membership and voting records, and ensures minutes are posted to the Association's website once approved. Debbie Sloane currently holds this combined role, with a term ending in 2027.

## Article 5 - Committees

### 5.1 Roads Committee

The Roads Committee plans the grading, paving and snow plowing of the Association's private roads, recommends road contractors such as Turner Excavating for grading and Hollis Plowing for snow removal, and oversees the 10-year paving plan. Frank Ito, a director, chairs the Roads Committee and is the point of contact for road emergencies at 555-0189. The committee brings any contract over a Board-set threshold, such as the proposed $85,000.00 paving of the lower half of Ridge Road, to the full Board and, where a special assessment would be required, to the Owners for a vote.

### 5.2 Trails and Barn Committee

The Trails and Barn Committee maintains the six miles of riding trails, the community barn, and the fishing lake, including clearing fallen trees from trails, scheduling the lake dam's five-year inspection, and managing the barn's booking calendar and the horse paddock waiting list. The committee reports to the Board on the condition of the trails and dam and recommends any maintenance budget needed for the coming year. Committee members are Owners appointed by the Board who are not required to be directors.

### 5.3 Architectural Review Committee

The Architectural Review Committee reviews Owner applications for exterior changes under Article 3 of the Declaration and the Architectural Design Guidelines, and reports its decisions to the Board. Where no separate committee is seated, the Board acts as the Architectural Review Committee itself. The Board appoints committee members, who should include at least one Owner familiar with the approved color palette and construction standards, and may remove or replace a committee member at any time.

## Article 6 - Management

### 6.1 Self-Management and the Administrator

Oak Ridge Estates is self-managed by its volunteer Board and committees rather than by an outside management company, assisted by a part-time administrator, currently Carol Benson, who keeps office hours Mondays and Thursdays from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. at the barn office, reachable at 555-0188 or admin@oakridge.example. The administrator handles routine correspondence, assessment billing and collection, vendor scheduling for contractors such as Hollis Plowing and Turner Excavating, posting of notices and minutes, and administering the barn booking calendar and the horse paddock waiting list, all under the Board's direction.

### 6.2 Fiscal Year and Books

The Association's fiscal year runs from January 1 through December 31, matching the annual assessment due date of January 31. The Treasurer prepares a proposed budget for the Board's review each fall, for adoption before assessment notices go out, and the Board reviews the reserve study and reserve fund balance as part of that process. The Association's financial books are kept on a cash basis and are available for the Treasurer's report at every Board meeting and the annual meeting.

## Article 7 - Records and Inspection

### 7.1 Association Records

The Association keeps this Declaration, these Bylaws, the Rules and Regulations and other adopted policies, Board and Owner meeting minutes, the membership and voting roll, financial records, and architectural review files as its official records. Minutes are posted to the Association's website once approved at the following meeting, and the current governing documents are available from the administrator at the barn office during posted hours.

### 7.2 Owner Access to Records

Any Owner may inspect the Association's records during the administrator's posted office hours, Mondays and Thursdays from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., by giving the administrator reasonable advance notice of which records the Owner wishes to see. The Association will not disclose another Owner's individual account balance, violation history, or personal contact information to a requesting Owner, except as summarized anonymously (for example, by Lot number) in meeting minutes or committee reports.

## Article 8 - Amendment of Bylaws

### 8.1 Amendment Procedure

These Bylaws may be amended by a majority vote of the Lots represented at any annual or special meeting of the Owners with proper notice of the proposed amendment's text, a lower threshold than the two-thirds vote the Declaration requires to amend itself, since these Bylaws govern internal procedure rather than land use. An amendment to these Bylaws takes effect immediately upon the vote unless the amendment states a later effective date, and the Secretary files the amended text with the Association's permanent records and posts it to the website.
