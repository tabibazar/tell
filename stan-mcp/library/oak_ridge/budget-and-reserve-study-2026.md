# Oak Ridge Estates 2026 Budget and Reserve Study Summary
<!-- type: budget; adopted: 2025-11-08 -->

The Board adopted this budget and reserve study summary at its November 8, 2025 meeting, ahead of assessment notices going out for the 2026 fiscal year. It presents the 2026 operating budget, the status of the reserve fund, and the findings of the Association's most recent reserve study covering the private roads, the lake dam, and other long-lived Common Area assets.

## Article 1 - 2026 Operating Budget

### 1.1 Income

The 2026 operating budget is funded entirely by the annual assessment of $1,450.00 charged to each of the Association's 58 Lots, for total income of $84,100.00. The Association does not currently charge separate amenity fees as operating income, apart from the community barn's per-event fee and deposit and the horse paddock monthly fee, both of which are held and spent outside the general operating budget as described in the Amenities, Private Road and Equestrian Policy. No special assessment is included in the 2026 budget.

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Annual assessment, 58 Lots at $1,450.00 | $84,100.00 |
| Total 2026 income | $84,100.00 |

### 1.2 Operating Expenses

The 2026 operating budget allocates its $84,100.00 in income across road maintenance, insurance, the lake and dam, trail and barn upkeep, administration, and a contribution to the reserve fund, with the two largest line items, road maintenance and grading and snow plowing, together making up $52,000.00 of the total:

The budget is balanced, with total expenses equal to total income of $84,100.00, and carries no planned deficit or surplus for the year.

## Article 2 - Reserve Fund

### 2.1 Current Reserve Balance

The Association's reserve fund held $211,000.00 as of the most recent financial statement, built up over the Association's history through annual contributions, occasional surpluses, and interest earned on the balance. The reserve fund is kept separate from the operating accounts described in Article 1 and is spent only on major, infrequent capital projects such as road paving, dam repair, or barn replacement, rather than on routine annual expenses.

### 2.2 Reserve Contributions and Funding Policy

The 2026 budget contributes $1,200.00 from operating income to the reserve fund, a modest amount relative to the current $211,000.00 balance because the fund has historically also grown through budget surpluses in years when actual road and plowing costs came in under budget. The Board reviews the reserve contribution each year as part of the budget process and may recommend a higher contribution in a future budget if the reserve study in Article 3 shows the fund falling behind projected road paving and dam costs.

### 2.3 Major Projects Funded From Reserves

The reserve fund is expected to fund the paving of the lower half of Ridge Road, estimated at $85,000.00 and on the Board's agenda for a vote at its November 14, 2026 meeting, without requiring a special assessment, since the Association's 10-year paving plan anticipated this project and the reserve fund can absorb it while remaining well above $120,000.00 afterward. The reserve fund similarly stands ready to cover an unplanned dam repair identified by the five-year dam inspection described in Article 3.

## Article 3 - Reserve Study Findings

### 3.1 Road Paving Cycle

The Association's reserve study calls for repaving each section of the private roads roughly every 15 years, tracked through the Association's 10-year paving plan, which schedules which section of road is due next and estimates its cost for reserve planning purposes. The lower half of Ridge Road, up for a paving vote at the November 2026 meeting, is the next section due under this cycle, with other sections scheduled across the plan's remaining years.

### 3.2 Lake Dam Inspection Cycle

The reserve study schedules a professional inspection of the fishing lake's dam every five years, with the cost and any recommended repair work drawn from the reserve fund rather than the annual operating budget's $6,500.00 lake and dam line item, which covers routine maintenance rather than a structural inspection. The most recent inspection cycle is reported at Board meetings as it comes due, including the report expected at the November 14, 2026 meeting.

### 3.3 Long-Range Outlook

Based on the 15-year road paving cycle and the five-year dam inspection cycle, the reserve study projects the Association will need to draw down the reserve fund for a major road or dam project roughly once every several years, interspersed with years of modest reserve growth from the operating budget's $1,200.00 contribution and any budget surplus. The Board revisits this reserve study roughly every three to five years to update its cost estimates for labor, paving materials and engineering inspections.
