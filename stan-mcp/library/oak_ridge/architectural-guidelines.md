# Oak Ridge Estates Architectural Design Guidelines
<!-- type: architectural; adopted: 1990-05-01; amended: 2019-09-01 -->

These Architectural Design Guidelines implement Article 3 of the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions and give Owners the detail needed to plan an exterior change: the submission process, review timeline, pre-approved items, the approved color palette, and the construction standards for fences, sheds, setbacks and similar structures. The Board adopted these Guidelines May 1, 1990 and amended them September 1, 2019 to add electric vehicle charging, drones and the current fourteen-color palette.

## Article 1 - Review Process

### 1.1 Submitting a Request

An Owner planning any exterior change listed in Section 8.1 of the Rules and Regulations submits a written architectural request to the Architectural Review Committee, available from the administrator or the Association's website, describing the proposed change, its location on the Lot, materials and colors (by name or code from the approved palette where applicable), and a sketch or photo where helpful. A request for a structure requiring a Hollis County building permit, such as a barn over a certain size, should note the permit's status, though the committee's architectural approval and the county's permit are separate steps and an Owner needs both before building.

### 1.2 Review Timeline and Deemed Approval

The committee must respond to a complete request within 30 days of its submission with an approval, a conditional approval, a request for more information, or a denial stating the specific Guideline the request fails to meet. If the committee does not respond within 30 days, the request is deemed approved as submitted. The 30-day clock restarts if the committee requests more information and the Owner takes more than a few days to supply it, since the clock runs against a complete application, not an incomplete one.

### 1.3 Appeals of Committee Decisions

An Owner who disagrees with the committee's denial or conditions may appeal in writing to the Board within 30 days of the decision. The Board reviews the committee's decision against these Guidelines and the Declaration, and may uphold it, reverse it, or grant a variance where the Owner shows the Guideline creates an unusual hardship for the specific Lot, such as unusual grade or tree cover, without materially affecting the neighborhood's appearance. The Board's decision on appeal is final short of legal action.

## Article 2 - Pre-Approved and Fast-Track Items

### 2.1 Repainting in an Existing Color

An Owner repainting a home in the same color it is already painted needs no new architectural approval, since the exterior appearance is not changing. An Owner is encouraged to confirm the exact color name and code with the committee beforehand if unsure which approved color the home currently carries, especially on an older home where the paint may have faded or the Association's palette has been renamed since the home was last painted.

### 2.2 Drought-Tolerant Landscaping Fast-Track

A landscaping plan that replaces lawn with drought-tolerant plantings suited to the well water every Lot relies on receives a fast-track review, typically answered well inside the standard 30-day period, given the community's interest in reducing well demand. A fast-track request should still include a basic plant list and a sketch showing which areas are being converted, since the committee is confirming the plan does not block a sight line or drainage path rather than vetting plant selection.

### 2.3 Solar Panels

A solar panel request receives fast-track attention because state law protects an Owner's right to install solar and the committee may not deny a request based on appearance. The committee's review is limited to confirming the proposed placement does not create a safety issue, block an easement, or interfere with a neighboring Lot's own future solar access, and the committee should acknowledge a solar request well before the 30-day deadline given these narrow grounds for any condition.

## Article 3 - Approved Exterior Color Palette

### 3.1 The Fourteen Approved Colors

The Architectural Review Committee maintains physical paint chips for the fourteen approved exterior colors, listed below by name and general description; the physical chip, not the description, controls where a shade is in question.

| # | Name | Description |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | Hollis Fieldstone | warm medium gray |
| 2 | Barn Red | deep red, for trim and barns |
| 3 | Saddle Brown | medium reddish brown |
| 4 | Sage | soft gray-green |
| 5 | Wheat | pale warm tan |
| 6 | Charcoal Ridge | dark neutral gray |
| 7 | Cedar Bark | deep warm brown |
| 8 | Oat | light warm beige |
| 9 | Stone Gray | cool light gray |
| 10 | Forest Hollow | dark green |
| 11 | Clay | muted terra cotta |
| 12 | Birch White | off-white with a warm cast |
| 13 | Moss | muted gray-green, darker and cooler than Sage |
| 14 | Slate Blue | soft blue-gray |

An Owner may paint a home, and any shed matching it, in any one of these fourteen colors without further committee review of the color choice itself, though the architectural request process in Article 1 still applies to a new color.

### 3.2 Matching Disputes and the Sage/Moss Question

The committee resolves a dispute over whether a color matches the approved palette by comparing the actual surface, or a sample of the paint used, against the committee's physical chips for Sage and Moss, the two colors Owners most often confuse because both read as a muted gray-green in ordinary daylight and in photographs. "Close to Sage" is not the same as Sage: Moss is a distinct, separately numbered color on the palette, noticeably cooler and darker than Sage when the two chips are held side by side, and a shed or home painted in a shade between the two, or in a color purchased as "Sage" from a retailer without matching the Association's specific chip and code, has not met the approved-palette requirement. An Owner should request the committee's chip and code before purchasing paint rather than relying on a paint store's own name for a similar-looking color.

## Article 4 - Structures, Materials and Setbacks

### 4.1 Fences: Materials and Height

A fence must be split-rail or board style in a natural wood tone or an approved palette color, with wire mesh permitted on the inside face for pets or livestock containment. A privacy-style, sight-blocking fence may not exceed 5 feet in height, including one used for a pool enclosure. A fence facing a neighboring Lot should be centered on the surveyed property line where practical, and its style should be consistent on both sides so neither Owner faces the fence's back side.

### 4.2 Sheds and Outbuildings: Size, Height and Placement

A shed may be no larger than 120 square feet and no taller than 10 feet, must sit behind the home rather than in front of or beside it, and must be painted to match the home's approved color. A barn or other structure larger than a shed is not subject to the 120-square-foot limit but requires both architectural approval and, typically, a Hollis County building permit, as with the 40-by-60-foot horse barn the committee reviews under separate requests from time to time.

### 4.3 Decks, Patios and Pools

A deck, patio, or pool requires architectural approval showing its size, materials and distance from property lines before construction begins. A pool or hot tub must also be enclosed by a fence meeting Hollis County's pool safety code, which may exceed the standard 5-foot fence height where the county code requires it; the committee will note any such exception on the approval. Decking and patio materials should be natural wood, composite in a muted tone, or stone rather than bright or reflective finishes.

### 4.4 Roof Materials

A replacement roof should use architectural asphalt shingle, standing-seam metal, or another material in a tone that blends with the home's existing palette and the wooded setting; bright or reflective roofing is discouraged. An Owner submitting a roofing request should include the manufacturer's color name so the committee can compare it against homes nearby and avoid two adjoining homes ending up in an unusually similar or clashing combination.

### 4.5 Setbacks from Property Lines

A new structure, including a shed, barn, deck, or fence post line, should sit at least 50 feet from the private road's edge and at least 25 feet from a side or rear property line, reflecting the large-lot character of Oak Ridge Estates, though the committee may approve a lesser setback where a Lot's shape or grade makes the standard setback impractical and the neighboring Lot is not materially affected. A setback variance follows the appeal process in Section 1.3.

## Article 5 - Site and Landscape Standards

### 5.1 Tree Removal

Removing a tree with a trunk over 6 inches in diameter, measured at chest height, requires architectural approval, given how central the wooded lot lines are to the community's character and to erosion control along the trails. A dead, diseased, or storm-damaged tree posing an immediate hazard may be removed right away, with the Owner notifying the committee and providing photos within a reasonable time afterward rather than waiting for prior approval.

### 5.2 Driveways and Culverts

A driveway and the culvert where it meets the private road are the Owner's responsibility to build and maintain, and any change to a driveway's width, surface or entry point requires architectural approval to confirm the culvert will handle road drainage properly. An Owner replacing a culvert should coordinate the timing with the Roads Committee chair where the work will affect road access or the grading schedule.
