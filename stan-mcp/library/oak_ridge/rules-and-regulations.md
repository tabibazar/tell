# Oak Ridge Estates Rules and Regulations
<!-- type: rules; adopted: 1985-06-01; amended: 2015-04-01, 2023-05-01 -->

These Rules and Regulations implement the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for day-to-day life in Oak Ridge Estates and apply to every Owner, tenant, household member and guest. The Board adopted the original Rules on June 1, 1985 and has amended them twice, most recently May 1, 2023, to keep pace with solar panels, electric vehicles, drones and other changes the 1985 Rules did not anticipate. Fines for breaking a rule in this document follow the Fine and Enforcement Policy.

## Article 1 - Vehicles, Parking and Roads

### 1.1 Resident and Guest Parking

Resident vehicles must park in a garage or driveway rather than on the private roads, which are narrow and shared with horses, hikers and cyclists on the adjoining trails. A guest may park a vehicle on the road shoulder for up to 72 hours; a vehicle left longer than 72 hours is treated as improperly stored and subject to a courtesy notice under the Fine and Enforcement Policy. Parking that blocks a mailbox cluster, a fire hydrant, or a neighbor's driveway is never allowed, regardless of the 72-hour window, and the roads chair or administrator may have such a vehicle towed at the owner's expense.

### 1.2 Boats, RVs, Trailers and Commercial Vehicles

A boat, recreational vehicle or trailer may stand in a driveway for up to 48 hours while actively loading or unloading for a trip, but may not be stored there longer term; longer-term storage of this kind of vehicle belongs behind the house or in a shed meeting the Architectural Design Guidelines. No commercial vehicle over one ton, such as a box truck or a vehicle carrying a business's name and equipment, may park overnight on a Lot or the private roads, though a commercial vehicle may make deliveries or service calls during the day. A horse trailer kept for an Owner's own horses is not a commercial vehicle for purposes of this rule.

### 1.3 Private Roads and Speed Limit

Oak Ridge Estates' roads are private roads owned and maintained by the Association, not Hollis County, and carry a speed limit of 20 miles per hour at all times, reflecting the horses, riders, and children who share them with vehicle traffic. No vehicle or hauled equipment weighing over 20 tons may use the private roads without the advance approval of the Roads Committee chair, currently Frank Ito at 555-0189, who times heavy loads around fresh grading or paving and can suggest the best route to a Lot under construction. Speeding or repeated heavy-load violations are enforced under the Fine and Enforcement Policy like any other rule.

### 1.4 Moving Trucks

A moving truck or trailer may load or unload at a Lot between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. An Owner planning a move should notify the administrator at least a week ahead so the private roads can be kept clear of parked vehicles and any heavy-truck routing under Section 1.3 can be arranged in advance. This notice is especially important during an active road grading or paving project, such as the October 2026 work on Ridge Road, when a moving truck's route may need to avoid the affected section.

## Article 2 - Pets and Livestock

### 2.1 Household Pets

A household may keep up to three pets. Dogs must be leashed while on the private roads, the riding trails, or any other Common Area, and an Owner must pick up and properly dispose of a pet's waste immediately rather than leaving it for later. Persistent barking is treated as a nuisance: the Board will issue two written warnings to the pet's Owner, and barking that continues after the second warning is a violation under the Fine and Enforcement Policy. Cats, including barn cats kept for rodent control, are counted toward the three-pet limit only if they live primarily indoors or are claimed as a household pet rather than working farm animals.

### 2.2 Horses

An Owner may keep up to two horses for every two acres in the Lot, so a 4-acre Lot may keep up to four horses and a 2-acre Lot up to two. Manure must be composted or stored at least 100 feet from any well, including a neighbor's well, and from the shore of the fishing lake, to protect the community's groundwater and surface water. Horses kept on a Lot are in addition to any horse an Owner keeps in the Association's shared paddocks under the Amenities, Private Road and Equestrian Policy. An Owner building a new barn or expanding pasture fencing needs architectural approval under Article 8 before starting construction.

### 2.3 Chickens

An Owner may keep up to twelve hens; roosters are not allowed anywhere in Oak Ridge Estates because of the noise they carry across large, open lots. A chicken coop or run is subject to the same architectural review as a shed under the Architectural Design Guidelines if it is a permanent structure, though a small, movable coop may qualify for a faster review. Hens should be enclosed or supervised well enough that they do not wander onto a neighboring Lot or the private roads.

### 2.4 Hunting

Hunting of any kind, with a firearm, bow, trap, or other means, is not allowed anywhere within Oak Ridge Estates, including on an Owner's own Lot, given the shared trails, paddocks and family use of the woods throughout the community. This rule applies to hunting for sport and to pest control involving trapping or shooting; an Owner with a specific wildlife problem should contact the administrator about humane removal options rather than hunting the animal.

## Article 3 - Exterior Appearance and Landscaping

### 3.1 Exterior Paint Colors

A home's exterior must be painted a color from the Association's approved palette of 14 colors, set out in the Architectural Design Guidelines. Repainting a home in the same color it is already painted needs no new approval, but any change to a different color, including a different color from within the approved palette, requires an architectural request and the committee's approval, or the running of the 30-day deemed-approval period, before painting begins. This rule applies to the home's siding, trim and shutters, and separately to any shed, which must match the home's approved color under Section 3.4.

### 3.2 Fences

Fences at Oak Ridge Estates must be split-rail or board style in natural, unpainted or earth-tone colors appropriate to the community's rural setting; wire mesh may be added on the inside of a rail or board fence to contain pets or livestock. A privacy-style fence, meaning a solid fence built to block sightlines rather than an open split-rail or board design, may not exceed 5 feet in height anywhere on a Lot, including around a pool, which must still meet the separate pool fencing requirement in Section 4.1. A fence facing a neighboring Lot is a shared responsibility of both neighbors to maintain, though the Association enforces only its style and height, and any new fence needs architectural approval before installation.

### 3.3 Landscaping and Lawn Care

Lawns must be kept under 6 inches and planting beds kept weeded and reasonably tidy. Removing any tree with a trunk over 6 inches in diameter requires architectural approval first, reflecting the wooded character the community was built around, though a dead or hazardous tree may be removed immediately with photos and notice to the committee afterward. Drought-tolerant landscaping is welcomed and receives a fast-track architectural review, typically well inside the standard 30-day period, in recognition of the well water every Lot relies on.

### 3.4 Sheds and Outbuildings

A Lot may have one shed, no larger than 120 square feet and no taller than 10 feet, located behind the home and painted to match the home's approved exterior color. A shed requires architectural approval before construction, including its proposed size, height, placement and color, even though it is smaller than a barn or other structure requiring a county permit. An Owner disputing whether a shed's color matches the home closely enough should refer to the Architectural Design Guidelines' palette names and codes, since a visual impression alone, such as a shed looking "close to Sage," is not how the committee determines a color match.

### 3.5 Signs

One "for sale" or "for rent" sign up to 6 square feet is allowed per Lot. Political signs may be posted no more than 45 days before an election and must be removed within 7 days after it. A security company sign may be placed near the home's entry door at any time. No other commercial, directional or decorative sign is allowed without architectural approval, except as otherwise permitted for a home-based farm stand under Article 6.

### 3.6 Flags

The United States flag, the state flag, and flags honoring any branch of the United States military may be flown at any time without architectural approval, on a pole of reasonable size for a residential lot. Any other flag, such as a seasonal, decorative, or collegiate flag, may be flown only from a pole no taller than the roofline of the home, and a permanent flagpole taller than the roofline requires architectural approval as a structure.

### 3.7 Holiday Decorations

Holiday decorations, including lights, inflatables and seasonal displays, may go up no more than 30 days before the holiday they celebrate and must come down within 15 days after. This rule applies to every recognized holiday throughout the year, not only winter holidays, and an Owner who wishes to display decorations for an extended personal event, such as a wedding, should contact the administrator in advance. Decorations should not create a hazard to drivers, riders or pedestrians on the private roads at night.

### 3.8 Satellite Dishes

A satellite dish under 1 meter in diameter is allowed wherever reception requires it, and an Owner should place it where it does not face the street if reception still works from a less visible location. No architectural approval is required for a dish meeting this size limit, though an Owner should still notify the administrator of its general location for the Association's records.

### 3.9 Solar Panels

Solar panels are allowed on any home or outbuilding, and state law protects an Owner's right to install them; the Architectural Review Committee cannot refuse a solar request based on appearance alone. An Owner must still submit a request before installing panels so the committee can note the proposed placement and confirm it does not conflict with a setback or an easement, and the committee must respond, or allow the 30-day deemed-approval period to run, promptly given the fast-track treatment solar requests receive.

### 3.10 EV Charging Equipment

A home electric vehicle charger installed inside the garage needs no architectural approval. A charger mounted outdoors, such as on an exterior wall or a freestanding post near the driveway, needs architectural approval for its placement, though not for the decision to install one, so the committee can confirm it does not interfere with sight lines on the private road or a neighbor's view.

## Article 4 - Pools, Hot Tubs and Common Facilities

### 4.1 Private Pools and Hot Tubs

A private pool or hot tub requires architectural approval before construction, including its location, size and any pool house or deck, and must be enclosed by a fence meeting Hollis County's pool safety code regardless of the 5-foot fence height limit elsewhere in these Rules. Breaking a pool-area rule, such as leaving a gate propped open, is enforced the same as breaking a clubhouse or shared-amenity rule under the Fine and Enforcement Policy, with a possible loss of access to shared Association amenities for repeat violations.

### 4.2 Smoking in Common Areas

Smoking is not allowed in the community barn, in or around the riding trail staging areas, or in any other enclosed Common Area space. This rule protects the barn for events such as the annual chili cook-off and keeps the shared trail corridors comfortable for riders and hikers with allergies or asthma. Smoking on an Owner's own Lot, outdoors and away from a shared structure, is not restricted by this rule.

### 4.3 Drones

A drone may not be flown over another Owner's home or Lot without that Owner's permission, and may not be flown over the fishing lake while anyone is using it, to avoid startling horses on the nearby trails or people fishing at the lake. An Owner may fly a drone over the Owner's own Lot, away from neighboring homes, the lake, and the riding trails, consistent with any applicable Federal Aviation Administration rules.

## Article 5 - Trash, Utilities and Property Maintenance

### 5.1 Trash and Recycling

Trash and recycling bins may be brought to the curb, meaning the end of the driveway, no earlier than 6 p.m. the evening before the Association's private hauler collects on Fridays, and must be brought back out of street view by 8 p.m. on collection day itself. Recycling follows the same Friday schedule as trash. An Owner who will be away on a collection day should arrange for a neighbor or the hauler to manage the bins rather than leaving them out past the 8 p.m. deadline.

### 5.2 Wells and Septic Systems

Every Lot in Oak Ridge Estates is served by a private well and septic system rather than county water and sewer, and each Owner is responsible for having the septic tank pumped at least once every four years by any licensed pumper. An Owner must keep the pumping receipt and provide a copy to the administrator for the Association's file, since a missing receipt is treated as a violation of this rule regardless of whether the system is actually failing. An Owner who cannot locate a past receipt should have the tank pumped and inspected promptly to create a current record.

### 5.3 Brush Burning

Brush or yard debris may be burned only with a current Hollis County burning permit, on a day without significant wind, and at a safe distance from the wooded trail corridors that run through much of the community. An Owner planning a burn should notify the administrator or the roads chair beforehand so riders and hikers on nearby trails are aware. Burning household trash, tires, or any material other than brush and yard debris is never allowed under this rule.

## Article 6 - Occupancy, Rentals and Home-Based Business

### 6.1 Short-Term Rentals

A rental of a home for fewer than 30 days is not allowed anywhere in Oak Ridge Estates, whether booked through an online platform or arranged privately, because the community's roads, trails, barn and paddocks are sized and insured for a stable residential population rather than frequent overnight guests. An Owner who hosts a short-term stay in violation of this rule is subject to a $500.00 fine per stay under the Fine and Enforcement Policy, due immediately, in addition to any other enforcement action.

### 6.2 Long-Term Leases

An Owner who leases a home for 30 days or longer must register the lease with the administrator within 10 days of its start, providing a copy of the signed lease. The registration lets the Association keep accurate contact information for the occupant of the Lot and confirms the tenant has received a copy of these Rules. A leasing Owner remains responsible for any violation or unpaid assessment the tenancy causes, and the Association will direct enforcement notices to the Owner even where the tenant is at fault.

### 6.3 Home Occupations and Farm Stands

A home-based business, including a small farm stand selling produce, eggs or preserves grown or made on the Lot, is allowed so long as it does not create traffic, parking, noise or outdoor storage noticeable from neighboring Lots or the road. A farm stand sign is allowed in addition to the "for sale" sign permitted under Section 3.5, but should be modest in size and taken down when the stand is not operating. A home occupation that grows into a source of neighbor complaints may be asked by the Board to scale back or relocate its visible activity.

## Article 7 - Noise and Community Events

### 7.1 Quiet Hours and Construction Noise

Quiet hours run from 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. every day, during which loud outdoor noise, including amplified music, should be kept to a minimum. Construction work and lawn equipment may run from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturdays, and may not run at all on Sundays, giving the community a predictable quiet day given the large lots and shared sound carried across open land. A contractor working on an Owner's Lot is bound by this schedule the same as the Owner.

### 7.2 Garage Sales

The Association holds two community-wide garage sales a year, one in May and one in September, advertised together so that shoppers can visit multiple Lots in one trip. An individual Owner who wants to hold a garage sale outside those two community dates needs the Board's permission first, mainly so the administrator can let neighbors know to expect extra traffic on the private roads that day.

## Article 8 - Architectural Review Process

### 8.1 When Review Is Required

Any change to the outside of a home or Lot, including a new paint color, a fence, a deck or patio, a new roof material, replacement windows, a shed or barn, a pool or hot tub, solar panels, or landscaping beyond planting within an existing bed, requires an architectural request to the Architectural Review Committee before work begins. The committee must respond within 30 days of a complete application; no response within 30 days counts as approval. The full submission process, pre-approved items, and the approved color palette are set out in the Architectural Design Guidelines.
