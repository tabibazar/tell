# Oak Ridge Estates Amenities, Private Road and Equestrian Policy
<!-- type: policy; adopted: 1997-04-01; amended: 2016-06-01 -->

This Policy governs the use of Oak Ridge Estates' shared amenities, the maintenance and rules of its private roads, and the keeping of horses and other livestock, reflecting the community's rural, large-lot character. The Board adopted this Policy April 1, 1997, when the riding trails and community barn were completed, and amended it June 1, 2016 to add the horse paddock waiting list procedure.

## Article 1 - Private Roads

### 1.1 Maintenance Responsibility

The Association is responsible for grading, paving and plowing the private roads throughout Oak Ridge Estates, funded through the operating budget's road maintenance and snow plowing line items and, for major repaving, through the reserve fund under the 10-year paving plan. A driveway culvert where it meets the private road is the Lot owner's responsibility to install and maintain, since a poorly maintained culvert can undermine the adjoining road surface the Association otherwise maintains.

### 1.2 Speed Limit and Heavy Vehicles

The speed limit on all private roads is 20 miles per hour, reflecting the horses, riders, cyclists and children who share the roads with vehicle traffic throughout the community. No vehicle or hauled load over 20 tons may use the private roads without the advance approval of the Roads Committee chair, currently Frank Ito, reachable at 555-0189, who can time a heavy load around fresh grading or paving and suggest routing that avoids the most vulnerable sections of road.

### 1.3 Snow Removal

Hollis Plowing, the Association's contracted vendor, plows the private roads once snowfall reaches 3 inches, under the snow plowing line item in the operating budget; driveway plowing is not included in the Association's road contract. An Owner who wants a driveway plowed may sign up for the Association's optional driveway plowing program, at $35.00 per storm, with sign-up due by November 15 each year so Hollis Plowing can plan its driveway route alongside the road route for the coming winter.

### 1.4 Road Emergencies

For a road emergency outside business hours, such as a fallen tree blocking a private road or a washed-out culvert after heavy rain, an Owner should call the roads chair's emergency line at 555-0189. For a medical, fire or police emergency, an Owner should call 911 and give the lot number and road name, since many private roads are not individually marked on standard mapping and a lot number helps responders find the right driveway quickly.

## Article 2 - Riding Trails

### 2.1 Trail Hours and Right of Way

The six miles of riding trails are open from dawn to dusk to horses, hikers and bicycles, but not to motorized vehicles of any kind, including off-road and electric bikes with a motor. On a shared trail, a rider does not yield to anyone, and everyone else, whether on foot or on a bicycle, yields to a horse, since horses can be startled by a sudden approach from behind or the side. A map of the trail system and the lot boundaries it crosses is available from the Trails and Barn Committee.

### 2.2 Trail Maintenance

The Association, through the Trails and Barn Committee, maintains the riding trails, including clearing a fallen tree that blocks a trail even where that section of trail crosses an Owner's own Lot. An Owner who notices storm damage, erosion, or a trail obstruction should report it to the administrator or directly to the Trails and Barn Committee so it can be scheduled for clearing, particularly before a community event such as the fall trail ride.

## Article 3 - Fishing Lake

### 3.1 Lake Hours and Catch Limits

The fishing lake is open from dawn to dusk. Bass must be caught and released, and an angler may keep up to five sunfish per day; swimming in the lake is not allowed at any time, to protect both swimmers and the fish population the Association stocks and manages. These limits apply to every Owner, tenant and guest fishing the lake, and the Trails and Barn Committee posts any seasonal change to the catch limits at the lake access point and on the Association's website.

### 3.2 Lake Dam

The Association owns and is responsible for the fishing lake's dam, which is professionally inspected every five years under the reserve study described in the 2026 Budget and Reserve Study Summary, with any recommended repair funded from the reserve fund rather than the annual operating budget. An Owner who notices a change in the dam, such as new seepage, a crack, or unusual erosion, should report it immediately to the administrator or the roads chair rather than waiting for the next scheduled inspection.

## Article 4 - Community Barn

### 4.1 Booking the Barn

The community barn may be booked for an Owner's private event of up to 100 people, for a fee of $50.00 plus a refundable $200.00 deposit, with bookings accepted up to 6 months in advance through the administrator. The Owner who books the barn is responsible for cleaning it up the same day as the event, after which the deposit is returned; a barn left uncleaned may result in the Association retaining some or all of the deposit to cover cleaning costs.

### 4.2 Barn Office and Administrator

The Association's part-time administrator, currently Carol Benson, keeps office hours at the barn office Mondays and Thursdays from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., reachable at 555-0188 or admin@oakridge.example, and is the point of contact for booking the barn, joining the horse paddock waiting list, registering a long-term lease, or submitting payment plan and architectural requests. Outside those posted hours, routine matters can be left by phone message or email for a response on the next office day.

## Article 5 - Horse Paddocks

### 5.1 Shared Paddocks and Fees

The Association maintains four shared horse paddocks available to Owners for $120.00 per month per horse, covering turnout space for an Owner whose own Lot is not set up for pasture or who needs supplemental turnout for an additional horse. A paddock is assigned to one Owner's horse at a time rather than shared among multiple horses, and the monthly fee is billed separately from the annual Lot assessment.

### 5.2 Waiting List

Because only four paddocks are available, a resident who wants a paddock joins a waiting list kept by the administrator and is offered the next open paddock in the order the list was joined. An Owner currently assigned a paddock who no longer needs it should notify the administrator promptly so the next Owner on the waiting list can move in without an unnecessary gap in paddock availability.

## Article 6 - Equestrian and Livestock Rules

### 6.1 Horses on Private Lots

An Owner may keep up to two horses for every two acres contained in the Lot, in addition to any horse the Owner keeps in the Association's shared paddocks under Article 5. Manure must be composted or stored at least 100 feet from any well, including a neighbor's, and from the shore of the fishing lake, to protect the groundwater and surface water the whole community relies on. A new barn, run-in shelter, or expanded pasture fencing requires architectural approval before construction begins.

### 6.2 Trail Etiquette and Liability

A rider using the Association's trails or paddocks does so at the rider's own risk, and the Association's master insurance policy covers the Association's liability for the Common Area itself rather than an individual rider's injury or a horse's conduct. Riders should keep horses under control around hikers, cyclists and other horses, announce their approach from behind at a walking pace, and report any damage a horse causes to trail footing or fencing to the Trails and Barn Committee so it can be repaired promptly.
