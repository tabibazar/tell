# Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Oak Ridge Estates
<!-- type: ccrs; adopted: 1985-03-15; amended: 2008-06-10 -->

This Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions (this "Declaration") was recorded March 15, 1985 by the original developer of Oak Ridge Estates and establishes the covenants, easements and restrictions that run with the land for all 58 Lots in the community. Oak Ridge Estates sits on roughly 2- to 5-acre lots along private roads in the rolling woods of Hollis County, with six miles of riding trails, a stocked fishing lake and a community barn held as Common Area for the benefit of all Owners. This Declaration was amended June 10, 2008 to update its assessment and architectural review provisions; except as amended, the 1985 text remains in force.

## Article 1 - Definitions

### 1.1 Association, Board and Officers

The Oak Ridge Estates Property Owners Association (the "Association") is the nonprofit association of all Owners responsible for administering this Declaration, the Bylaws, and the Rules and Regulations. The "Board" means the Association's Board of Directors, currently three directors elected at large by the Owners. The Board selects a President, who presides at meetings and represents the Association, and a Treasurer, who keeps the Association's financial accounts; the Board may combine the Secretary's recordkeeping duties with the Treasurer's office. The Association is self-managed by volunteer directors and committee members, assisted by a part-time administrator who keeps office hours at the community barn and handles day-to-day correspondence, billing and vendor scheduling on the Board's behalf.

### 1.2 Owner and Lot

An "Owner" is the record title holder of a "Lot," meaning one of the 58 numbered parcels of 2 to 5 acres platted within Oak Ridge Estates. Where a Lot has more than one titleholder, all titleholders together hold one membership and one vote, to be cast as they agree among themselves. "Owner" includes a contract purchaser in possession but does not include a mortgagee holding title only as security. Each Lot carries one well and one septic system, and is intended for a single detached home, together with any barn, shed, paddock or other structure approved under Article 3. A Lot's boundaries, easements and any shared driveway or culvert arrangements are as shown on the recorded plat on file with Hollis County.

### 1.3 Common Area and Amenities

The "Common Area" consists of the private roads, the six miles of riding trails, the stocked fishing lake and its dam, the community barn, the four shared horse paddocks, the cluster mailbox structures, and the landscaped strips the Association maintains along the roads, together with the land each sits on. The Common Area is held for the shared use and enjoyment of all Owners and their household members and guests, subject to the hours and rules set in the Association's Amenities, Private Road and Equestrian Policy. No part of the Common Area may be sold, encumbered or removed from common use except by the amendment procedure in Article 8. The Association, not any individual Owner, holds title to and insures the Common Area under Article 6.

### 1.4 Declarant and Architectural Review Committee

The "Declarant" was the original developer of Oak Ridge Estates, built out in phases between 1985 and 2010; the Declarant's special rights to appoint directors and approve plans ended once the last phase sold, and the Association has been fully owner-controlled since that time. The "Architectural Review Committee" (also called the ARC or the committee) is a standing committee appointed by the Board under Article 3 and the Bylaws to review applications for exterior changes against this Declaration and the Architectural Design Guidelines. References in this Declaration to "the committee" mean the Architectural Review Committee unless the context plainly means the Roads Committee or the Trails and Barn Committee.

## Article 2 - Use Restrictions

### 2.1 Residential Use and Home Occupations

Each Lot shall be used only for single-family residential purposes, together with the keeping of household pets and the livestock permitted under Section 2.2 and the operation of a home occupation that does not create traffic, noise, or outdoor storage noticeable from neighboring Lots or the road. Home occupations are expressly permitted, including small farm stands selling produce, eggs or preserves grown or made on the Lot. An Owner operating a home business shall not post commercial signage beyond what the Rules and Regulations allow, employ more than occasional on-site help, or store commercial inventory or equipment outside an approved shed or barn. The Board may require an Owner to cease a home occupation that becomes a nuisance to neighboring Lots.

### 2.2 Animals and Livestock

Owners may keep up to three household pets, and this Declaration separately permits the keeping of livestock suited to the community's large-lot, rural character: up to two horses for every two acres of a Lot, and up to twelve hens with no roosters. Manure from horses or other livestock shall be composted or stored at least 100 feet from any well or from the shore of the fishing lake. Dogs must be leashed while on Common Area and their waste picked up immediately. No Lot may be used for commercial boarding, breeding, or raising of animals for sale, and hunting is not permitted anywhere within Oak Ridge Estates, including on an Owner's own Lot.

### 2.3 Leasing and Occupancy

An Owner may lease a Lot for residential occupancy, but rentals shorter than 30 days are prohibited; Oak Ridge Estates is not a short-term rental community and an Owner who books a stay shorter than 30 days through any platform or private arrangement is subject to the fine set in the Fine and Enforcement Policy. A lease of 30 days or longer must be registered with the administrator within 10 days of its start, with a copy of the signed lease identifying the tenant and the lease term. A leasing Owner remains responsible for the tenant's compliance with this Declaration, the Rules and Regulations, and all amenity rules, and for any assessments, fines or costs the tenancy generates.

### 2.4 Nuisances, Noise and Hunting

No Owner shall permit any activity on a Lot that is a nuisance, hazard or unreasonable source of noise, dust, or odor to neighboring Lots. Quiet hours run from 10 p.m. to 7 a.m.; construction and lawn equipment may run 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturdays, and not at all on Sundays. Outdoor burning of brush or debris is allowed only with a current Hollis County burning permit, on a calm day, and away from the wooded trail corridors. Hunting with any firearm, bow, or trap is prohibited everywhere in Oak Ridge Estates, consistent with the community's shared trails, paddocks and family use of the Common Area.

### 2.5 Vehicles, Parking and Private Roads

Vehicles shall be parked in garages or driveways; guests may park on the private roads for up to 72 hours, and boats, recreational vehicles and trailers may stand on a driveway for up to 48 hours while loading or unloading. No commercial vehicle over one ton may be parked overnight on a Lot or the roads. Because Oak Ridge Estates' roads are private and maintained by the Association rather than Hollis County, the speed limit on all private roads is 20 miles per hour, and no vehicle or equipment over 20 tons may use the roads without the advance approval of the Roads Committee chair, who can route heavy loads to avoid fresh grading or paving.

### 2.6 Signs, Flags and Exterior Displays

One "for sale" or "for rent" sign up to six square feet is permitted per Lot, along with a security company sign near the entry door. Political signs may go up no more than 45 days before an election and must come down within 7 days after. The United States flag, the state flag, and flags honoring any branch of the military may be flown at any time; any other flag must fly from a pole no taller than the roofline of the home. Holiday decorations may go up no more than 30 days before the holiday and must come down within 15 days after. The Rules and Regulations give the full detail on sizes, placement and exceptions for all of these displays.

## Article 3 - Architectural Control

### 3.1 Architectural Review Required

No Owner may make an exterior change to a Lot, including a change of paint color, a new or replaced fence, a deck or patio, a new roof material, replacement windows, a solar installation, a shed or barn, a pool, or any landscaping beyond planting within existing beds, without first submitting a request to the Architectural Review Committee and receiving its approval or allowing the review period to run under Section 3.3. This requirement protects the wooded, large-lot character the community was platted around and ensures new structures fit the approved palette and setback standards in the Architectural Design Guidelines. A change made without approval is subject to the $250.00 fine and restoration order set in the Fine and Enforcement Policy.

### 3.2 The Architectural Review Committee

The Architectural Review Committee reviews every application under Section 3.1 against this Declaration and the Architectural Design Guidelines then in effect. The Board appoints the committee's members, who need not be directors, and the Board may act as the committee itself when no separate committee is seated. The committee may approve an application as submitted, approve it with conditions, request more information, or deny it with written reasons tied to a specific standard in the Guidelines; looks alone is not a valid reason to deny a solar installation, which state law protects. The committee keeps a written record of every application and decision, which the administrator files with the Owner's account.

### 3.3 Review Period and Deemed Approval

The Architectural Review Committee shall respond to a complete application within 30 days of its submission. If the committee does not respond within 30 days, the application is deemed approved as submitted, and the Owner may proceed, though the Owner remains bound by all other restrictions in this Declaration, including setbacks, height limits, and the approved color palette. An incomplete application, such as one missing a site sketch, a material sample or a contractor's plan, does not start the 30-day period until the Owner supplies the missing item. Fast-track review applies to drought-tolerant landscaping and to solar panel notices, which the committee should acknowledge well inside the 30-day window.

### 3.4 Variances and Appeals

An Owner who disagrees with a committee decision may ask the Board, in writing and within 30 days of the decision, to review it. The Board may uphold the committee, reverse it, or grant a variance from a specific Guideline standard where the Owner shows the standard works an unusual hardship on the Lot's shape, grade or tree cover and the variance will not noticeably affect neighboring Lots or the road. A variance applies only to the Lot and application it was granted for and does not change the Guidelines for any other Owner. The Board's decision on appeal is the Association's final decision short of legal action.

## Article 4 - Maintenance Responsibilities

### 4.1 Owner's Maintenance Obligations

Each Owner is responsible for maintaining, repairing and replacing, at the Owner's expense: the home's roof (though the Board may require repair of a roof that is visibly failing), the driveway and its culvert where it meets the private road, trees located on the Lot (including the cost of removing one that falls onto Common Area), the water heater, furnace and all plumbing inside the home, the well and septic system serving the Lot, and pest control inside the home. An Owner's fence facing a neighboring Lot is maintained equally by both neighbors, though the Association enforces only the fence's style and height under the Architectural Design Guidelines, not its day-to-day repair.

### 4.2 Association's Maintenance Obligations

The Association is responsible for grading, paving, and plowing the private roads; maintaining the landscaping in the Common Area through its landscaping contractor; maintaining the street lights (Owners should report an outage to the administrator); maintaining the street trees planted in the strip between the sidewalk and the road; maintaining the six miles of riding trails, including clearing a fallen tree across a trail even where the trail crosses an Owner's Lot; inspecting and maintaining the fishing lake and its dam; owning and maintaining the cluster mailbox structures, though the United States Postal Service controls the locks and issues keys; repairing trip hazards in any sidewalk fronting a home; and controlling pests in the Common Area.

### 4.3 Shared, City and Other Responsibilities

A fence built along the Common Area becomes the Association's maintenance responsibility once installed with architectural approval; if an Owner built an unapproved fence along Common Area, that Owner remains responsible for it. Hollis County's water utility maintains the water main and sewer line in the road right-of-way, and the county maintains storm drains, though Owners should report a blocked drain to the administrator, who will forward it to the county. An Owner clears snow and fallen leaves from the sidewalk fronting the Owner's own home even though the Association repairs trip hazards in that same sidewalk. Driveway snow removal is each Owner's choice and expense; the Association's optional driveway plowing sign-up is described in the Amenities, Private Road and Equestrian Policy.

## Article 5 - Assessments and Liens

### 5.1 Annual Assessment

Each Lot is assessed one annual assessment of $1,450.00, equal to a monthly rate of $120.83, due in full on January 31 of each year. The annual assessment funds the Association's operating budget, which covers private road maintenance and plowing, the riding trails, the fishing lake and dam, the community barn, and the Association's master insurance policy. The Board adopts the coming year's budget and assessment amount before the annual assessment becomes due, and notifies all Owners of the amount and due date by mail or email at least 30 days in advance. An Owner who wishes to pay quarterly rather than in one payment may request a payment plan under the Collection Policy.

### 5.2 Late Charges and Interest

An assessment not paid in full by January 31 is late, and a $75.00 late fee is added to the Owner's account for any balance still unpaid after March 1. Interest continues to accrue on any unpaid assessment, late fee, or fine under the schedule in the Collection Policy, which also describes the Association's quarterly payment plan option. Late charges and interest are assessments against the Lot in the same way as the annual assessment itself and are secured by the lien described in Section 5.3. Nothing in this section limits the separate fines the Board may levy under the Fine and Enforcement Policy for violations unrelated to payment.

### 5.3 Assessment Lien

If an account remains delinquent for six months, the Association may record a lien against the Lot for the unpaid assessments, late charges, interest, fines and the Association's reasonable costs of collection, including attorney's fees. The lien remains on the Lot's title until the balance is paid in full or the Board agrees to release it under a payment arrangement, and it is enforceable in the same manner as a mortgage lien under Hollis County law. The Association will record a release of lien within a reasonable time after an Owner satisfies the balance secured by it. Filing a lien does not relieve an Owner of an outstanding fine or of future assessments as they come due.

### 5.4 Special Assessments

In addition to the annual assessment, the Board may call for a special assessment to fund a capital project, such as road reconstruction beyond what the reserve fund covers, storm damage repair, or dam repairs identified by the five-year dam inspection. A special assessment requires the affirmative vote of Owners holding at least a majority of the Lots voting at a meeting called for that purpose, where a quorum of 30 percent of Lots (18 Lots) is present in person or by proxy. The notice of the meeting must state the project, its estimated cost, and the proposed per-Lot share. The Association has not needed a special assessment in recent years because its 10-year paving plan and reserve fund anticipate major road and dam work in advance.

## Article 6 - Insurance

### 6.1 Master Policy

The Association carries a master insurance policy providing liability coverage for the private roads, the riding trails, the community barn, and the fishing lake and dam, protecting the Association and its Owners against claims arising from an accident on the Common Area. The premium for this policy is a line item in the annual operating budget and is reviewed each year as part of the budget process described in the Bylaws. The master policy does not cover the inside or contents of any home, any structure on a Lot, or an Owner's personal liability for incidents on the Owner's own property; those risks are the Owner's responsibility under Section 6.2.

### 6.2 Owner's Insurance Obligation

Each Owner must carry the Owner's own homeowner's insurance covering the home, any barn, shed or other structure on the Lot, and the Owner's personal liability, including liability arising from horses, chickens or other animals kept on the Lot. The Association recommends, though does not require, that an Owner with a barn, horses, or a private pool or hot tub carry an umbrella liability policy given the Common Area trails and paddocks neighboring many Lots. An Owner planning new construction, such as a barn requiring a county building permit, should confirm coverage begins before the structure is occupied or used. The Association is not liable for an Owner's failure to carry adequate insurance.

## Article 7 - Enforcement

### 7.1 Violations and Notice

The Board enforces this Declaration, the Bylaws and the Rules and Regulations through the notice, cure and fine procedure set out fully in the Fine and Enforcement Policy, beginning with a no-cost courtesy notice and a 14-day cure period for most first-time violations. This Declaration does not separately restate that schedule, but an Owner is bound by it the same as by the substantive rules themselves. The Board or its appointed hearing panel decides enforcement actions after giving the Owner an opportunity to be heard, except where a violation is established by an objective record, such as a missing septic pumping receipt, that leaves no factual dispute to hear.

### 7.2 Right of Entry

The Association, through its contractors or committee members, may enter the Common Area portion of any Lot, such as a trail easement or a drainage swale, to perform grading, trail clearing, dam inspection, or similar maintenance described in Article 4, after reasonable notice except in an emergency such as a washed-out culvert or a tree blocking a road. This right of entry does not extend to a home or any enclosed structure on a Lot without the Owner's separate consent or a court order. The Association will repair any damage its maintenance work causes to an Owner's landscaping or fencing outside the Common Area easement.

### 7.3 Legal Remedies

In addition to fines, the Board may seek a court order requiring an Owner to comply with this Declaration, may remove or correct a violation at the Owner's expense and assess the cost against the Lot, and may recover its reasonable attorney's fees and costs from an Owner found in violation after a hearing. These remedies are in addition to, not instead of, the lien and collection remedies in Article 5 and the Collection Policy. The Board will generally pursue the notice-and-cure process in the Fine and Enforcement Policy before resorting to legal action, except where a violation threatens safety, such as an unmaintained septic system or an unpermitted burn near the wooded trails.

## Article 8 - Amendment

### 8.1 Amendment Procedure

This Declaration may be amended by an instrument signed by Owners holding at least two-thirds of the 58 Lots (39 Lots) and recorded with Hollis County, or by the same two-thirds vote of Lots cast at a meeting called for that purpose with proper notice of the proposed amendment's text. The Bylaws, which govern the Association's internal operation rather than the land itself, may be amended by the lower threshold set out in the Bylaws. No amendment may reduce an Owner's voting rights or single out one Lot for different treatment than other similarly situated Lots without that Owner's consent.

### 8.2 Amendment History

This Declaration was originally recorded March 15, 1985 as part of the development of Oak Ridge Estates. It was amended June 10, 2008 to update the architectural review timeline to the current 30-day, deemed-approval standard, to add the short-term rental restriction in Section 2.3, and to update the assessment lien procedure in Section 5.3 to its current six-month trigger. Owners may obtain a copy of the original 1985 Declaration and the 2008 amendment from the administrator or from the Hollis County land records office; both documents remain part of the chain of title for every Lot in the community.

## Article 9 - General Provisions

### 9.1 Duration

This Declaration runs with the land and binds every Owner, together with that Owner's heirs, successors, tenants and assigns, for as long as Oak Ridge Estates exists as a residential community. No Owner may opt out of this Declaration by deed language, by abandonment of a Lot, or by failing to use the Common Area, and the covenants in this Declaration survive the sale or transfer of any Lot.

### 9.2 Severability and Conflicts

If a court holds any provision of this Declaration invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions continue in full force. Where a specific policy adopted under this Declaration, such as the Rules and Regulations or the Fine and Enforcement Policy, states a number, deadline or procedure in more detail than this Declaration, the more detailed policy controls so long as it does not contradict this Declaration; where the two genuinely conflict, this Declaration controls. Any reference in this Declaration to a dollar amount, deadline or percentage matches the figure currently adopted by the Board in the relevant policy unless this Declaration states otherwise.
