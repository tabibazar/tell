# Oak Ridge Estates Fine and Enforcement Policy
<!-- type: enforcement; adopted: 2011-02-01; amended: 2024-03-01 -->

This Fine and Enforcement Policy sets out how the Board enforces the Declaration, the Bylaws, the Rules and Regulations, and the Architectural Design Guidelines, including the notice and cure periods, the fine schedule, and an Owner's right to a hearing and an appeal. The Board adopted this Policy February 1, 2011 and amended it March 1, 2024 to add the short-term rental fine and update the continuing-violation daily rate.

## Article 1 - Notice and Cure

### 1.1 Courtesy Notice

The first time an Owner is found in violation of most rules, the Association sends a courtesy notice describing the violation and giving the Owner 14 days to fix it; no fine accompanies a courtesy notice. The courtesy notice identifies the specific rule violated, such as a parking or trash-timing rule, and states what correcting the violation looks like, for example moving a vehicle or bringing bins back out of view. An Owner who fixes the violation within the 14 days has no fine and no further action is taken, though the notice remains in the Owner's file in case the same issue recurs.

### 1.2 Second Notice, Same Issue

If the same violation recurs or is not fixed within the courtesy notice's 14-day cure period, the Association sends a second notice carrying a $50.00 fine and a further 14 days to cure. The second notice is sent for the same underlying issue, not a new, unrelated violation, which would instead start again at the courtesy-notice stage under Section 1.1. An Owner who believes the second notice was sent in error may request a hearing under Article 3 before the fine is added to the account.

### 1.3 Hearing Before the Board

If a violation continues after the second notice's cure period, the Board schedules a hearing, with reasonable written notice to the Owner of the date, time and the specific violation at issue, at which the Owner may present evidence and explanation before the Board decides whether the violation continues and, if so, what fine or remedy applies. Hearings are typically held as part of a regular Board meeting, with the hearing itself conducted before the public portion of the meeting where the Owner prefers privacy.

## Article 2 - Fine Schedule

### 2.1 Continuing Violations

A violation the Board finds continuing after a hearing carries a fine of $25.00 per day until it is fixed, capped at $1,000.00 total for that violation. The daily fine begins accruing the day after the hearing decision unless the Board sets a short additional cure period in its decision, and stops accruing once the Owner corrects the violation and the Association confirms the correction. An Owner facing a continuing fine should contact the administrator as soon as the violation is corrected so the daily accrual can be verified and stopped promptly.

### 2.2 Unapproved Architectural Changes

An exterior change made without the required architectural approval carries a $250.00 fine, in addition to the cost of restoring the Lot to its prior approved condition, or of obtaining approval after the fact where the committee would have approved the change if asked first. The Owner has 30 days from the violation notice to either restore the prior condition or submit a complete architectural request; if the committee denies that after-the-fact request, the Owner must restore the prior condition within a further 30 days.

### 2.3 Short-Term Rentals

An Owner who rents a home for fewer than 30 days is fined $500.00 for that stay, due immediately rather than after a cure period, since a short-term stay cannot be "cured" once it has happened. Each separate short-term stay is a separate violation carrying its own $500.00 fine, and a pattern of repeated short-term rentals may also support a hearing on continuing violation fines under Section 2.1 or legal action under the Declaration.

### 2.4 Pool and Clubhouse Violations

Breaking a rule governing the community barn, the pool fencing requirement, or another shared amenity carries a $100.00 fine and the possible loss of amenity access for up to 30 days, such as suspension of barn booking privileges or paddock access, depending on the severity and whether the Owner has a prior violation of the same kind. The Board decides the length of any access suspension as part of its decision under Article 1, considering whether the violation created a safety risk to other Owners or their animals.

## Article 3 - Hearings and Appeals

### 3.1 Requesting a Hearing

An Owner who wishes to contest a notice or a fine may request a hearing before the Board in writing within 14 days of the notice, stating the basis for the dispute. The Board schedules the hearing within 30 days of the request, at a regular or special Board meeting, and the Owner may bring documents, photos, or another person to speak on the Owner's behalf.

### 3.2 Appealable and Non-Appealable Notices

Most notices may be contested through the hearing process in Section 3.1, where the facts of the violation, or whether it has been fixed, are genuinely in dispute. A notice based on an objective record the Owner controls, such as a missing septic pumping receipt under the wells and septic rule, is not treated as appealable in the usual sense, because the record either exists or it does not; instead of a hearing on the facts, the Owner should simply provide the missing receipt or documentation by the stated deadline to resolve the notice.

### 3.3 Board Decision and Record

The Board issues its hearing decision in writing within 14 days of the hearing, stating whether the violation is found, confirmed as continuing, or dismissed, and the fine or remedy that applies. The decision becomes part of the Owner's permanent violation record, which the Association keeps confidential from other Owners except in an anonymized, by-Lot-number summary reported to the Board or at a meeting.

## Article 4 - Collection of Fines

### 4.1 Fines Added to Owner's Account

A fine assessed under this Policy is added to the Owner's assessment account and is due on the same terms as a regular assessment, including the late fee and interest described in the Collection Policy if it is not paid promptly. An unpaid fine, like an unpaid assessment, counts toward the six-month threshold after which the Association may record a lien against the Lot under the Declaration.
