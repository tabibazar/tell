# Architectural and Renovation Guidelines for Harbor View Condominium Association
<!-- type: architectural; adopted: 2020-03-04 -->

These guidelines explain how an Owner at Harbor View Condominiums requests approval for an alteration, renovation, or exterior-visible change, what the Renovation Review Committee looks at, and how long review takes. They implement Article 4 of the Condominium Declaration. Because Harbor View is a 22-storey tower rather than a community of detached homes, these guidelines focus on interior renovations touching shared systems, balcony and window alterations, and finish standards, rather than on lot-based items like fences or landscaping.

## Article 1 - When Approval Is Required

### 1.1 Changes that need approval

An Owner must obtain approval before beginning any renovation that touches plumbing, electrical wiring, load-bearing or demising walls, or flooring; before replacing or altering a window or an exterior-facing door; before mounting a satellite dish anywhere other than the preferred balcony location described in the Rules and Regulations; and before installing any equipment visible from outside the Unit, such as balcony-mounted solar equipment. A cosmetic change entirely inside a Unit that does not touch these systems, such as repainting interior walls or replacing furniture, does not require approval.

### 1.2 Application process

An Owner submits a renovation or alteration application to management through BuildingLink, describing the scope of work, listing the licensed and insured contractor who will perform it, and attaching any plans or product specifications the Committee needs to evaluate the request, such as an underlayment's acoustic rating. The Renovation Review Committee reviews complete applications and responds within 30 days; if the Committee has not responded within 30 days of a complete submission, the application is deemed approved under the Declaration.

### 1.3 Deposit for qualifying renovations

Any renovation touching plumbing, electrical systems, walls, or floors requires a refundable $1,000 deposit, collected before work begins, to cover any damage to Common Elements, hallways, elevators, or neighboring Units caused by the work. The deposit is returned within 30 days of the Committee confirming the work is substantially complete and that no chargeable damage occurred. The deposit is paid once per renovation project rather than per trade, even where several contractors work on the same project in sequence.

## Article 2 - Review Standards

### 2.1 Work hours

All approved renovation and alteration work must be performed on weekdays between 9:00 am and 5:00 pm. This window protects quiet hours and weekend use of the building for Owners who are not renovating, and it is enforced regardless of the scope or size of the project. A contractor found working outside these hours is treated the same as an unapproved architectural change for enforcement purposes, and the Owner is notified immediately so the work can stop.

### 2.2 Acoustic flooring standard

Any hard-surface flooring, including tile, hardwood, laminate, or engineered wood, must be installed over an acoustic underlayment rated Impact Insulation Class (IIC) 50 or better. The application must state the underlayment's manufacturer and rating; the Committee may require a post-installation test at the Owner's expense if a neighbor later reports impact noise, and may require correction if the standard was not met.

### 2.3 Contractor requirements

Every contractor performing renovation work at Harbor View must be licensed for the trade involved and carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance, with a certificate provided to management before work begins. The Owner remains responsible for the contractor's compliance with the work-hour restriction and for any damage to Common Elements beyond what the deposit covers. Management keeps a copy of each contractor's certificate on file with the renovation application and may bar a contractor from the building if its insurance lapses mid-project.

### 2.4 Balcony and exterior-visible finishes

Where a Unit's entry door, balcony divider, or other surface visible from a hallway or from outside the building is to be refinished in a new colour, the Owner must select from the Association's approved palette of 14 exterior-visible colours, available from management, or request Board approval for an exception consistent with the building's overall appearance. Refinishing in the same colour that is already approved for that element does not require a new application.

### 2.5 Satellite dishes and drones

A satellite dish under 1 meter in diameter is pre-approved for placement on a Unit's own balcony, preferably facing away from the street, and needs no separate application; a dish an Owner wants mounted anywhere else, such as a railing extension facing the water, requires Committee review for structural attachment and appearance. Drone use from a balcony is addressed in the Rules and Regulations rather than this architectural process, since it does not involve a physical alteration.

### 2.6 Solar equipment

An Owner may request approval for solar equipment serving the Unit, most practically balcony-mounted panels given the tower's shared roof. Consistent with state law protecting solar access, the Committee's review is limited to confirming safe placement and structural attachment and may not refuse a request based on appearance alone; the Committee notes the approved placement in the Unit's file. Because the building's main roof is a Common Element housing shared mechanical equipment, an Owner should not expect approval for rooftop-mounted panels serving a single Unit.

## Article 3 - Renovation Review Committee Procedure

### 3.1 Committee role

The Renovation Review Committee, described in the Bylaws, is the body that reviews every application involving plumbing, electrical systems, walls, or floors, confirms the contractor's license and insurance, and recommends approval, conditional approval, or denial to the Board. Routine window, door-finish, and satellite-dish requests not touching a Unit's internal systems may be handled directly by management under standing Committee guidelines, with any disputed request referred to the full Committee.

### 3.2 Appeals of a Committee decision

An Owner who disagrees with the Renovation Review Committee's decision may appeal to the full Board by submitting a written request within 30 days of the decision. The Board reviews the appeal at its next regular meeting, may request additional information from the Owner or the Committee, and its decision on an architectural appeal is final. An Owner may continue to use the Unit normally while an appeal is pending, but must not begin the disputed work until the appeal is resolved in the Owner's favor.

### 3.3 Deemed approval

If the Committee does not respond to a complete application within 30 days, the requested change is deemed approved and the Owner may proceed, provided the work still complies with the work-hour, contractor, deposit, and acoustic standards in this document. An Owner relying on deemed approval should keep a copy of the submitted application and proof of its submission date.
