# 2026 Budget and Reserve Study Summary for Harbor View Condominium Association
<!-- type: budget; adopted: 2025-11-19 -->

This document summarizes the annual operating budget the Board adopted in November 2025 for the 2026 fiscal year, together with the findings of the 2025 reserve study. It is provided so Owners can see how the $612.00 monthly assessment is spent and how the Association is funding major future repairs, including the elevator modernization now proposed as a special assessment.

## Article 1 - Operating Budget

### 1.1 Total budget and revenue

The 2026 annual operating budget is $1,322,000.00. Owner assessments at $612.00 per month across all 180 Units produce $1,321,920.00 in annual assessment revenue; the remaining $80.00 comes from other income such as net EV charger proceeds, application and transfer fees, and interest earned on operating funds, bringing total revenue to $1,322,000.00, matching total budgeted expenses. The Board adopted this budget in November 2025 following the standard annual process of reviewing the prior year's actual spending, the managing agent's cost projections, and the reserve study's recommended funding level before setting the monthly assessment for the coming year.

### 1.4 Concierge and staffing

The largest single budget line, $410,000.00, covers the 24-hour concierge desk staffed by Harbor Front Desk Ltd under contract, which provides package handling, visitor registration, and after-hours emergency contact at 555-0171. This line also covers the on-site presence coordinated with Coastal Residential's general manager, Elena Varga, who oversees daily building operations from the lobby-level management office during weekday business hours, 9:00 am to 5:00 pm.

### 1.5 Insurance and utilities

The $220,000.00 insurance line pays the premium for the Association's master policy covering the building structure and Common Elements, subject to its $25,000 deductible per claim, as described in the Condominium Declaration. The $190,000.00 utilities line covers water, gas, and common-area electricity for the building, including the lobby, hallways, elevators, garage lighting, pool, gym, and rooftop terrace; it does not include the EV chargers' electricity, which is billed separately to Owners who use them at $0.28 per kilowatt-hour.

### 1.6 Reserve contribution and vendor contracts

The 2026 budget allocates $230,000.00 to the reserve fund, building toward the fully funded target implied by the current $1,180,000.00 reserve balance representing 54 percent funding, as found in the 2025 reserve study. The budget also funds the elevator maintenance contract with LiftCo at $45,000.00 and the cleaning contract with Sparkle Commercial at $85,000.00, both recurring vendor agreements the Board renews annually.

## Article 2 - Reserve Study

### 2.1 Current reserve funding

The Association's most recent reserve study, completed in 2025, found a reserve fund balance of $1,180,000.00, representing approximately 54 percent of the fund's fully funded target of roughly $2,185,000.00. The study identifies the elevators and the garage membrane as the two largest anticipated future expenses facing the reserve fund. A reserve study of this kind is typically refreshed every three to five years, and the Board expects to commission the next update once the elevator and membrane projects are underway.

### 2.2 Elevators

The reserve study flags the building's elevators as a major near-term capital item, consistent with the Board's current proposal to modernize two passenger elevator cars at a cost of $640,000.00, to be funded through a special assessment rather than the reserve fund, since the reserve study did not anticipate the full replacement cost within its current funding level. LiftCo, the Association's elevator service vendor, has provided the estimate underlying this project.

### 2.3 Garage membrane

The reserve study also identifies the garage's waterproofing membrane as a significant future reserve expense, given the building's age since its 2008 construction and the wear a below-grade parking structure experiences from vehicle traffic and moisture. The Board is monitoring the membrane's condition through routine inspections and expects to budget a dedicated reserve project for it in a future fiscal year, separate from the elevator special assessment.

### 2.4 Reserve funding plan

The Board's approach to the gap between the current 54 percent funding level and a fully funded reserve is to increase annual reserve contributions gradually, as reflected in the $230,000.00 contribution in the 2026 operating budget, while funding the immediate elevator project through a special assessment so that the reserve fund itself is not depleted by a single large capital project.

## Article 3 - Special Assessment for Elevator Modernization

### 3.1 Proposed special assessment

The Board has proposed a special assessment to fund the $640,000.00 elevator modernization project, covering the two passenger elevator cars (Elevator A and Elevator B) serving the tower; the building's separate service elevator, used for moves and deliveries, is not part of this modernization scope. Divided evenly across the building's 180 Units, this amounts to approximately $3,550.00 per Unit, consistent with the per-unit figure the Board has communicated to Owners.

### 3.2 Payment schedule

If approved, the special assessment would be payable over 12 months, at approximately $296.00 per month per Unit in addition to the regular $612.00 monthly assessment, rather than as a single lump sum. The special assessment and its payment schedule require a membership vote under the Bylaws before it may be levied, and is expected to be presented formally at the October 28, 2026 Board meeting.

### 3.3 Why a special assessment rather than reserves

The Board has chosen a special assessment rather than drawing from reserves because the reserve fund, at 54 percent funding, is already below its target level, and using a large share of it for the elevator project would leave insufficient reserves for the garage membrane and other items the 2025 reserve study identified. A special assessment keeps the reserve fund intact for its broader long-term purpose while still funding the elevator project on the timeline LiftCo has recommended.
