# Collection Policy of Harbor View Condominium Association
<!-- type: collection; adopted: 2020-03-04 -->

This policy explains when monthly assessments and other charges are due at Harbor View, what late fees apply, when an account is referred to collections, and how an Owner may arrange a payment plan. It implements Article 5 of the Condominium Declaration and applies to all Owners, including those who lease their Unit to a tenant, since the Owner remains responsible for the account regardless of who occupies the Unit.

## Article 1 - Due Dates and Late Fees

### 1.1 Monthly due date

The monthly assessment of $612.00 is due on the first day of each month. Owners may pay by autopay enrollment through BuildingLink, by one-time online payment, or by another method management makes available, and are encouraged to use autopay to avoid missing the due date. An Owner who changes bank accounts should update autopay promptly in BuildingLink to avoid a declined payment and the late fee that follows.

### 1.2 Late fee

An assessment not received by the 10th of the month incurs a late fee of $50.00, added automatically to the Owner's account. For example, an Owner who misses the October 1 due date and has not paid by October 10 owes the $612.00 assessment plus the $50.00 late fee, for a total of $662.00, until paid. The late fee applies once per missed due date and does not compound further within the same month beyond the single $50.00 charge.

### 1.3 What the assessment covers

The monthly assessment funds water, gas, building insurance, the 24-hour concierge, amenity operation and maintenance, and the Association's reserve contributions, as described in the 2026 Budget and Reserve Study. Because these costs are bundled into the single monthly assessment, Owners do not receive separate utility bills for water or gas, only the garage EV charger usage, which is billed separately at $0.28 per kilowatt-hour.

## Article 2 - Payment Plans

### 2.1 Requesting a payment plan

An Owner facing temporary difficulty paying an assessment may request a payment plan of up to three months from management, spreading the past-due balance, including any late fee, across the agreed months in addition to the Owner's regular ongoing monthly assessment. A payment plan request should be made before the account is referred to collections under Article 3, though management may still agree to a plan afterward at its discretion.

### 2.2 Terms of a payment plan

A payment plan is a written agreement between the Owner and management specifying the monthly amount, the number of months (up to three), and the consequence of missing a scheduled payment, which is typically immediate referral to collections without further notice. An Owner who completes a payment plan in full returns to normal billing with no further collection action on the resolved balance.

## Article 3 - Collections

### 3.1 Referral to collections

An account more than 60 days past due is referred to the Association's collection process, which may include a formal demand letter, a lien filed against the Unit under Article 5 of the Declaration, and recovery of the Association's collection costs, including reasonable attorney's fees, from the Owner. Referral to collections does not require a separate Board vote for each account; it follows automatically from the 60-day threshold under this policy.

### 3.2 Interest and costs added to the account

Once an account is in collections, interest accrues on the unpaid balance at the rate the Board sets from time to time, and the Owner is responsible for the Association's reasonable costs of collection, including any filing fee for a lien and attorney's fees incurred pursuing the debt. These amounts are added to the Owner's account balance and must be paid along with the original assessment and any late fee before the account is considered current.

### 3.3 Liens

The Association's lien against a Unit for unpaid assessments, as described in the Condominium Declaration, is recorded with the county once an account reaches collections and remains in place until the balance, including interest and collection costs, is paid in full. A recorded lien can affect an Owner's ability to sell or refinance the Unit until it is released, which the Association does promptly upon payment in full.

### 3.4 Foreclosure as a last resort

If a lien remains unpaid for an extended period and other collection efforts have failed, the Association may pursue foreclosure of the lien as permitted by state law, as a last resort after collection letters, payment plan opportunities, and interest and cost accrual have not resolved the account. The Board reviews any account proceeding to this stage before authorizing foreclosure proceedings.

## Article 4 - Owner and Tenant Responsibility

### 4.1 Owner remains responsible

The Owner of a Unit is responsible for the full assessment and any late fee, interest, or collection cost regardless of whether the Unit is owner-occupied or leased to a tenant under a registered lease. A tenant's failure to pay rent to the Owner does not excuse or delay the Owner's assessment obligation to the Association, and the Association does not bill a tenant directly even when it knows the Unit is rented.

### 4.2 Closing and transfer of ownership

When a Unit is sold, any unpaid assessment, late fee, fine, or collection cost on the account is typically settled at closing, and the Association provides an estoppel statement showing the account balance upon request from the Owner, the buyer, or their closing agent. A new Owner takes title subject to the Association's recorded lien for amounts a prior Owner did not pay, except as state law otherwise limits lien priority against a first mortgage holder.
