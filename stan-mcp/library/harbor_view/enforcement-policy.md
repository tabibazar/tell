# Fine and Enforcement Policy of Harbor View Condominium Association
<!-- type: enforcement; adopted: 2020-03-04; amended: 2026-01-12 -->

This policy explains how Harbor View Condominium Association enforces the Condominium Declaration, Bylaws, and Rules and Regulations, including the notice and cure periods an Owner receives before a fine is imposed, the hearing process, the fine schedule, and how an Owner may appeal. It applies to violations by an Owner, tenant, household member, guest, or contractor, with the Owner ultimately responsible for payment.

## Article 1 - Notice and Cure Process

### 1.1 Courtesy notice

For most rules, the first time a violation is reported, management sends the Owner a courtesy notice describing the violation and giving 14 days to correct it, with no fine imposed. The courtesy notice identifies the specific rule violated, such as a balcony item that does not belong there or an unregistered pet, and explains what correction management expects to see.

### 1.2 Second notice

If the same violation continues or recurs after the courtesy notice's 14-day cure period, management issues a second notice carrying a $50.00 fine and a further 14 days to cure. The second notice is the point at which most violations are expected to be resolved, since the Owner has already been informed of the issue and the applicable rule, and it is added directly to the Owner's account for collection.

### 1.3 Continuing violations and hearings

A violation that continues after a hearing is held under Article 2 is fined at $25.00 per day until corrected, capped at $1,000.00 total for that violation. The daily fine begins accruing from the date of the hearing decision, not from the original notice, and stops accruing once management confirms the violation has been corrected. Once the $1,000.00 cap is reached, the Board may pursue legal remedies such as an injunction rather than continuing to fine the Owner further.

## Article 2 - Hearings

### 2.1 Right to a hearing

An Owner who disputes a notice or wishes to contest a fine before it is imposed or continues may request a hearing before the Board, or a committee the Board designates for that purpose, by notifying management in writing within the cure period stated on the notice. The hearing request does not pause the cure period unless the Board grants an extension.

### 2.2 Hearing procedure

At a hearing, the Owner may present evidence and explain the circumstances of the alleged violation, and management presents the basis for the notice. The Board (or its designated committee) decides whether the violation occurred and, if so, whether the fine schedule in Article 3 applies as written or whether circumstances justify a different outcome, and issues its decision in writing within 14 days of the hearing.

## Article 3 - Fine Schedule

### 3.1 General fine schedule

Except where a specific violation carries its own fine under this Article, violations follow the general schedule: a courtesy notice with no fine and a 14-day cure period for a first-time issue; a second notice carrying a $50.00 fine and a further 14-day cure period if the issue continues; and, if a hearing confirms a continuing violation, a fine of $25.00 per day, capped at $1,000.00 total, until the violation is corrected.

### 3.2 Unapproved architectural change

A change made to the exterior of the building, a Limited Common Element, or a Unit's internal systems requiring approval under Article 4 of the Declaration, made without first obtaining that approval, is fined $250.00, and the Owner must also restore the Common Element or Unit to its approved condition at the Owner's expense. The Owner has 30 days from the notice to complete the restoration before further enforcement, including a continuing-violation hearing, may follow.

### 3.3 Short-term rental

A Unit rented for a stay shorter than the 12-month minimum lease term is fined $500.00 per stay, with no cure period, because the unauthorized occupancy cannot be reversed once it has taken place; the Owner must end the arrangement immediately upon receiving notice from management, and a repeated short-term rental is treated as a new, separate violation each time it occurs.

### 3.4 Pool, gym, or clubhouse rule violation

A violation of the rules governing the pool, gym, rooftop terrace, or party room, such as exceeding the posted guest limit or using the gym under the age of 16, is fined $100.00 and may result in the loss of amenity access for up to 30 days, at the Board's discretion based on the severity and history of the violation. Because a balcony grill creates a fire-safety risk comparable in seriousness to a pool or clubhouse violation, it is fined on this $100.00 tier starting with a Unit's second notice for that issue, rather than the general $50.00 second-notice amount, reflecting the heightened risk open flame poses in a high-rise building.

## Article 4 - Appeals

### 4.1 Appeal of a hearing decision

An Owner may appeal a hearing decision to the full Board, if the hearing was conducted by a designated committee, by submitting a written appeal within 14 days of the decision. The Board reviews the record from the original hearing and may request additional information, and its decision on appeal is final under the Association's internal process, without prejudice to any right the Owner has under state law.

### 4.2 Payment of fines

A fine imposed under this policy is charged to the Owner's account and is collected in the same manner as an assessment under the Collection Policy, including late fees and, if unpaid, referral to collections after 60 days. Payment of a fine does not excuse the underlying violation if it has not actually been corrected, and management continues to track the violation as open until it confirms the condition no longer exists.

## Article 5 - Enforcement Discretion

### 5.1 Board discretion

The Board may, for good cause shown, reduce, waive, or extend the cure period for a fine under this policy, particularly for a first violation involving an Owner's demonstrated hardship or a misunderstanding promptly corrected. This discretion does not apply to the short-term rental fine in Section 3.3, which is not subject to a cure period by its nature. An Owner seeking this kind of discretionary relief should raise it at a hearing under Article 2 rather than waiting for a fine to escalate further.

### 5.2 Multiple violations

An Owner with multiple open violations, or a pattern of repeated violations of the same rule across different periods, may be referred directly to a hearing under Article 2 without a further courtesy notice, at the Board's discretion, where the pattern shows the courtesy process has not achieved compliance. This discretion is used sparingly and is reserved for Owners who have already received at least one courtesy notice for the same or a related rule in the preceding year.
