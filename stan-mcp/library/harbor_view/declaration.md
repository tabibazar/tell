# Condominium Declaration for Harbor View Condominium Association
<!-- type: declaration; adopted: 2008-06-12; amended: 2020-03-04 -->

This Declaration was recorded in 2008 by the original developer to create Harbor View Condominiums, a 22-storey waterfront tower in Bayport containing 180 Units, and to submit the property to condominium ownership under state law. It sets out the definitions, property rights, use restrictions, maintenance responsibilities, assessment and lien powers, insurance requirements, and enforcement and amendment procedures that bind every Owner, tenant, and guest. Harbor View Condominium Association (the "Association") administers this Declaration through its Board of Directors and its managing agent, Coastal Residential.

## Article 1 - Definitions

### 1.1 Unit

A "Unit" means one of the 180 individually owned residential spaces within the tower, identified by its unit number (for example, Unit 1704 or Unit 905), bounded by the unfinished interior surfaces of its perimeter walls, floors, and ceilings. A Unit includes the airspace enclosed by those boundaries and the fixtures serving only that Unit, such as interior doors, cabinetry, plumbing fixtures beyond the shut-off valve, and the in-unit heat pump. It does not include the structural walls, floors, or ceilings themselves, nor any pipe, conduit, or duct that serves more than one Unit, all of which are Common Elements.

### 1.2 Common Elements

"Common Elements" means all portions of the property other than the Units and the Limited Common Elements, including the tower's structure, foundation, exterior walls and windows, roof, hallways, stairwells, elevators, lobby, the 24-hour concierge desk, the garage (except assigned parking spaces, which are Limited Common Elements), mechanical rooms, the 4th-floor pool and gym, the rooftop terrace, the party room, and the grounds. The Association owns and maintains the Common Elements for the benefit of all Owners, who hold an undivided interest in them appurtenant to their Unit.

### 1.3 Limited Common Elements

"Limited Common Elements" means those parts of the Common Elements reserved for the exclusive use of one Unit, even though the Association owns and insures them. Each Unit's balcony, its assigned garage parking space, and its assigned storage locker are Limited Common Elements. An Owner may use and must keep clean the Limited Common Elements appurtenant to the Unit, but may not alter, enclose, or structurally modify them without architectural approval under Article 4.

### 1.4 Owner and Occupant

An "Owner" is the person or persons holding record title to a Unit, whether resident or not. An "Occupant" is any person living in a Unit, including a tenant under a registered lease or a member of an Owner's household. Owners remain responsible to the Association for the conduct of their Occupants and guests and for any violation, fine, or damage an Occupant or guest causes, regardless of whether the Owner was present.

### 1.5 Board and Association documents

The "Board" means the Board of Directors of Harbor View Condominium Association, elected under the Bylaws. "Governing Documents" means this Declaration, the Bylaws, the Rules and Regulations, and any architectural or policy document the Board adopts consistent with them. Where a Governing Document conflicts with this Declaration, this Declaration controls, and where the Bylaws conflict with a Rule or policy, the Bylaws control.

## Article 2 - Property Rights and Use Restrictions

### 2.1 Residential use

Each Unit shall be used only as a private residence for the Owner, the Owner's family, or a tenant under a lease meeting the requirements of Article 8, except that a home office is permitted under Section 2.6. No Unit may be used for retail, manufacturing, or any use that creates noise, odor, or traffic noticeable to other Owners, and no Unit may be subdivided or combined with another Unit without an amendment to this Declaration.

### 2.2 Smoking

No smoking of any kind, including tobacco, cannabis, or vaping devices, is permitted anywhere in the building, including inside Units, on balconies, in hallways, the garage, or any other Common Element or Limited Common Element. This restriction protects the shared ventilation serving all 180 Units and is enforceable the same as any other use restriction in this Declaration, including against a guest or tenant whose Owner remains responsible for the violation.

### 2.3 Balconies

Balconies are for plants and outdoor furniture only. No grills of any kind, no laundry, no storage of boxes or equipment, and nothing hung, mounted, or placed on the railings is permitted. A gas or charcoal grill on a balcony is treated as a fire-safety violation under the Fine and Enforcement Policy because the tower's fire suppression and egress design does not accommodate open flame above the ground floor. Furniture and plantings must not extend past the railing or drip onto the balcony below.

### 2.4 Pets

An Owner or Occupant may keep up to two pets per Unit, and any dog must weigh under 50 pounds. All pets must be registered with management through BuildingLink and must be leashed whenever outside the Unit, including in hallways and the lobby. Pet owners must use the service elevator when it is free rather than the passenger elevators, to keep shedding and odor out of finished common corridors, and must clean up after their pets immediately in any Common Element.

### 2.5 Leasing

No Unit may be leased for a term shorter than 12 months, and no short-term or transient rental of any length is permitted. Every lease must be registered with management before the tenant occupies the Unit, using the form available through BuildingLink, and must include the tenant's name and contact information. The Owner remains responsible for the tenant's compliance with all Governing Documents and for all assessments, whether or not the tenant pays rent.

### 2.6 Home occupations

An Owner or Occupant may conduct a home office from a Unit provided it generates no exterior signage, no more than two client or customer visits to the building per day, and no employee who is not a resident of the Unit working on site. A home occupation that increases traffic through the lobby, requires deliveries beyond normal residential volume, or generates noise audible in the hallway is a nuisance under this Article and may be restricted by the Board.

### 2.7 Signs, dishes, and drones

A satellite dish under 1 meter across may be installed on a balcony where reception requires it, preferably facing away from the street side of the building; a dish requiring any other location needs architectural approval. Drones may not be flown over another Unit's balcony or over the pool or rooftop terrace while either is in use. Signs are limited to a single notice in a Unit's own window not exceeding the size customary for residential use; no sign may be placed in a hallway, on a balcony railing, or in any Common Element without Board permission.

### 2.8 Nuisance and quiet hours

No Owner or Occupant may create a nuisance that unreasonably interferes with another resident's use and enjoyment of a Unit or the Common Elements, including excessive noise, noxious odors, or hazardous conditions. Quiet hours run from 10:00 pm to 8:00 am daily, during which hard-surface noise, music, and construction noise must be kept inaudible in neighboring Units. Hard-surface flooring installed after the date of this Declaration must meet the acoustic standard in Article 4.

## Article 3 - Maintenance and Repair Responsibilities

### 3.1 Owner responsibilities within a Unit

An Owner is responsible, at the Owner's own expense, for maintaining and repairing everything within the Unit's boundaries as defined in Section 1.1, including interior plumbing from the shut-off valve inward, the Unit's heat pump, interior walls, flooring, fixtures, and appliances. An Owner must also maintain pest control inside the Unit; the Association is responsible for pest control only in Common Elements.

### 3.2 Association responsibilities

The Association maintains, repairs, and replaces the Common Elements, including the tower structure, roof, elevators, hallways, the garage, the pool, gym, rooftop terrace, and party room, and the exterior of the windows, which the Association's cleaning contractor washes twice a year, in April and October. The Association cleans and maintains hallways, elevators, and the garage continuously as part of the assessments described in Article 5.

### 3.3 Balconies

The Association owns the structural balcony slab, railing, and waterproofing as a Limited Common Element, and is responsible for their structural maintenance, including the inspections required every five years under Section 3.4. The Owner of the Unit to which a balcony is appurtenant must keep it clean and free of prohibited items under Section 2.3, and must report any visible cracking, rust, or water intrusion to management promptly.

### 3.4 Balcony inspections

Because balconies are structural cantilevers exposed to weather, the Association inspects every balcony for cracking, corrosion of embedded steel, and waterproofing failure at least once every five years, consistent with the building's age and reserve study recommendations. An Owner must provide access for a scheduled inspection, and the Association will give at least 14 days' notice except in an emergency. Repairs identified by an inspection are a Common Element expense unless caused by the Owner's alteration or neglect.

### 3.5 Water damage between Units

When water escapes from a pipe, fixture, or appliance within one Unit and damages another Unit or a Common Element, the Owner whose pipe, fixture, or appliance caused the escape is responsible for the resulting damage, and that Owner's homeowner's insurance ordinarily settles the claim with the affected Owner's insurer. The Association's master policy responds only to damage to Common Elements and the building structure, not to the cost of repairing an Owner's finishes, personal property, or another Owner's Unit.

### 3.6 Renovations affecting shared systems

Any work inside a Unit that touches plumbing, electrical wiring, load-bearing or demising walls, or flooring requires a renovation application approved in advance, use of a licensed and insured contractor, and a refundable $1,000 deposit held by the Association against damage to Common Elements or neighboring Units. Work may proceed only on weekdays between 9:00 am and 5:00 pm. The Renovation Review Committee reviews applications involving plumbing, walls, or floors before work begins, under the procedure in Article 4.

## Article 4 - Architectural Control

### 4.1 Approval required

No Owner may alter the exterior appearance of the building or make a visible change affecting the Common Elements or Limited Common Elements without prior written approval, including changes to windows, balcony enclosures, exterior-facing doors, satellite dish placement outside the preferred location, and any structural, plumbing, electrical, or flooring renovation inside a Unit. The Renovation Review Committee reviews applications and must respond within 30 days of a complete submission; if no response is given within 30 days, the application is deemed approved.

### 4.2 Approved colours

Where a Unit's entry door, balcony divider, or other element visible from a Common Element is to be repainted or refinished in other than the original finish, the Owner must choose from the Association's approved palette of 14 colours, available from management, or obtain Board approval for an exception. Repainting or refinishing in the same, already-approved colour does not require a new application.

### 4.3 Flooring standard

Any hard-surface flooring installed in a Unit above the ground floor, including tile, hardwood, laminate, or engineered wood, must be installed over an acoustic underlayment rated Impact Insulation Class (IIC) 50 or better, to limit impact noise transmitted to the Unit below. The renovation application must include the underlayment's rating, and the Renovation Review Committee may require a post-installation acoustic test at the Owner's expense if a complaint arises.

### 4.4 Renovation application and deposit

An Owner proposing a renovation that touches plumbing, electrical systems, walls, or floors must submit a renovation application identifying the licensed, insured contractor, the scope of work, and the proposed schedule, and must pay a $1,000 refundable deposit before work begins. The deposit is returned within 30 days of substantial completion, less the cost of repairing any damage to Common Elements, hallways, elevators, or neighboring Units caused by the work or its contractors. Work is permitted only on weekdays from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm.

### 4.5 Solar and energy equipment

An Owner may request approval to install solar equipment serving the Owner's Unit, and the Renovation Review Committee may not refuse a request based on appearance alone, consistent with state law protecting solar access; the Committee's review is limited to confirming placement and structural attachment. Because Harbor View's Units do not have individual rooftop access, a solar request is typically limited to balcony-mounted equipment and is reviewed case by case.

## Article 5 - Assessments and Liens

### 5.1 Monthly assessments

Each Owner shall pay a monthly assessment of $612.00, due on the first day of each month, to fund the Association's operating budget and reserve contributions described in Article 6. The monthly assessment covers water, gas, building insurance, the 24-hour concierge, amenities, and reserve funding, so Owners do not receive separate bills for those shared services. Assessments may be paid by autopay through BuildingLink or by other methods management makes available.

### 5.2 Late fees and collections

An assessment not received by the 10th of the month incurs a late fee of $50.00. An account more than 60 days past due is referred to collections under the Collection Policy, which may include a lien against the Unit. Payment plans of up to three months may be arranged with management for an Owner experiencing temporary hardship, as described further in the Collection Policy.

### 5.3 Special assessments

The Board may levy a special assessment for a capital expense not funded by reserves, such as the elevator modernization project described in the 2026 Budget and Reserve Study, after the notice and vote required by the Bylaws. A special assessment is allocated among Units in proportion to each Unit's ownership share and may be paid in installments the Board approves, such as the 12-month schedule proposed for the elevator project.

### 5.4 Assessment liens

The Association has a lien against each Unit for unpaid assessments, late fees, interest, and the Association's collection costs, including reasonable attorney's fees, from the date the assessment becomes due. The lien is recorded and enforced as described in the Collection Policy and survives a sale of the Unit to the extent state law provides, except as to a first mortgage holder's priority for amounts accruing before the mortgage holder's notice.

## Article 6 - Insurance

### 6.1 Master policy

The Association maintains a master insurance policy covering the building structure and all Common Elements, including the lobby, hallways, elevators, garage, pool, gym, rooftop terrace, and party room, subject to a $25,000 deductible per claim. Premiums for the master policy are a Common Expense included in every Owner's monthly assessment and are among the largest line items in the annual budget.

### 6.2 Owner's HO-6 policy

Each Owner must maintain an HO-6 condominium unit-owner's policy covering the interior finishes, fixtures, and personal property within the Unit that the master policy does not cover, together with loss-assessment coverage of at least $25,000 to protect against the Owner's pro-rata share of the master policy's deductible if a claim affects the Owner's Unit. Management may request proof of an Owner's HO-6 policy at any time, and a mortgage lender typically requires one as a condition of financing.

### 6.3 Claims between the Association and Owners

If damage affects both Common Elements and a Unit's interior, the Association's master policy and the Owner's HO-6 policy each respond to the portion of the loss within their coverage, and the two insurers coordinate on apportioning the deductible. An Owner who fails to maintain HO-6 coverage remains personally responsible for any loss-assessment amount the Association charges back for that Owner's Unit.

## Article 7 - Enforcement

### 7.1 Authority to enforce

The Board may enforce this Declaration, the Bylaws, and the Rules and Regulations against any Owner, Occupant, or guest through notices, hearings, fines, and suspension of amenity access, following the procedures and fine schedule set out in the Fine and Enforcement Policy. The Board may also pursue legal action, including an injunction, where a violation is not resolved through the notice and hearing process.

### 7.2 Owner responsibility for others

An Owner is responsible for a violation committed by the Owner's tenant, family member, guest, or contractor as if the Owner had committed it, and any fine or cost may be assessed against the Owner's account and collected as an assessment under Article 5. This includes damage a contractor causes during a renovation beyond the $1,000 deposit held under Section 4.4.

## Article 8 - Leasing and Occupancy Restrictions

### 8.1 Minimum lease term

No Owner may rent or lease a Unit for an initial term of less than 12 months, and no short-term or vacation rental is permitted regardless of the platform or method used to arrange it. An Owner who leases a Unit must provide the Association with a copy of the lease or a management-approved summary, the tenant's name, and emergency contact information before occupancy begins.

### 8.2 Short-term rental violations

A Unit rented for a stay shorter than the 12-month minimum is a short-term rental violation under this Declaration, fined at $500.00 per stay as set out in the Fine and Enforcement Policy, with no cure period because the violation cannot be undone once the stay has occurred; the Owner must also terminate the arrangement immediately upon notice. A repeated short-term rental by the same Owner is treated as a new, separate violation each time it occurs, not a single continuing one.

## Article 9 - Amendments

### 9.1 Amendment procedure

This Declaration may be amended by a vote of Owners representing at least the percentage of total ownership interest required by state condominium law and the original recording, at a meeting called for that purpose or by written ballot, following the notice and quorum procedures in the Bylaws. An amendment is effective upon recording with the county and is binding on all Owners and their successors.

### 9.2 Board rulemaking between amendments

Between amendments to this Declaration, the Board may adopt, amend, or repeal Rules and Regulations and policies, such as the Fine and Enforcement Policy or the Collection Policy, provided they are consistent with this Declaration and the Bylaws and are adopted at an open meeting with notice to Owners. A Rule adopted by the Board carries the same enforceability as this Declaration until superseded or repealed.
