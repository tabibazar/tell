# Insurance Summary for Harbor View Condominium Association
<!-- type: insurance; adopted: 2020-03-04 -->

This summary explains, in plain terms, what the Association's master insurance policy covers, what an Owner's own HO-6 policy must cover, and how the two interact when a claim arises. It restates, for easy reference, the insurance requirements set out in Article 6 of the Condominium Declaration and is not a substitute for reading an Owner's own policy or consulting an insurance agent.

## Article 1 - The Master Policy

### 1.1 What the master policy covers

The Association's master insurance policy covers the building's structure and all Common Elements, including the lobby, hallways, elevators, garage, mechanical systems, the pool and gym, the rooftop terrace, and the party room, against fire, water damage, windstorm, and other insured perils. The policy also covers the structural balcony slabs and railings, since balconies are Limited Common Elements the Association owns, even though an individual Owner has exclusive use of the balcony appurtenant to the Owner's Unit.

### 1.2 The $25,000 deductible

The master policy carries a $25,000.00 deductible per claim. When a covered loss occurs, the Association pays this deductible from its operating or reserve funds in the first instance, but where the loss originated in or primarily affected a specific Unit, the Association may assess the deductible, or a portion of it, back to the responsible Owner, which is why Owner loss-assessment coverage under Article 2 matters.

### 1.3 What the master policy does not cover

The master policy does not cover an Owner's interior finishes such as flooring, cabinetry, or fixtures installed beyond the Unit's original condition, the Owner's personal property, or any liability arising from activity inside a Unit. These are the Owner's own responsibility to insure under an HO-6 policy, described in Article 2, and an Owner who assumes the master policy covers these items risks an uninsured loss.

## Article 2 - The Owner's HO-6 Policy

### 2.1 Required coverage

Every Owner at Harbor View must maintain an HO-6 condominium unit-owner's insurance policy covering the Unit's interior finishes, built-in improvements, fixtures, and the Owner's personal property, since the master policy does not extend to these items. An HO-6 policy also typically provides personal liability coverage for incidents occurring inside the Unit, and an Owner who rents the Unit to a tenant should confirm the policy still covers the Owner's own finishes and liability while the Unit is occupied by someone else.

### 2.2 Loss-assessment coverage

In addition to the coverage described in Section 2.1, every Owner's HO-6 policy must include loss-assessment coverage of at least $25,000.00, matching the master policy's deductible, so that if the Association assesses an Owner for a share of the master policy's deductible following a covered loss, the Owner's own insurer can respond to that assessment rather than the cost falling entirely on the Owner directly.

### 2.3 Proof of insurance

Management may request proof of an Owner's current HO-6 policy, including its loss-assessment coverage amount, at any time, and a mortgage lender financing the purchase of a Unit typically requires evidence of HO-6 coverage as a condition of closing. An Owner who allows HO-6 coverage to lapse remains personally responsible for any loss-assessment amount the Association charges to the Unit, regardless of insurance status.

## Article 3 - How Claims Are Handled

### 3.1 Damage confined to one Unit's interior

When damage, such as a water leak from a Unit's own plumbing, affects only that Unit's interior finishes and personal property, the responsible Owner's HO-6 policy is the primary coverage, since the master policy does not respond to interior finishes or contents. The Association's role is typically limited to confirming the source of the loss and any Common Element repair needed, such as to pipes within a wall.

### 3.2 Damage affecting a neighbor

When water or another insured peril originating in one Unit damages a neighboring Unit, the Owner whose pipe, fixture, or appliance caused the loss is responsible, and the matter is usually settled between the two Owners' HO-6 insurers, consistent with the maintenance responsibility set out in the Condominium Declaration. The Association's master policy is not the first source of recovery for this kind of Unit-to-Unit damage.

### 3.3 Damage affecting Common Elements

When a loss affects the building structure or Common Elements, such as a fire affecting a hallway or a leak damaging the garage below, the Association's master policy responds to the Common Element repair, subject to the $25,000.00 deductible, which the Association may assess back to an Owner whose Unit was the source of the loss. An Owner's HO-6 loss-assessment coverage is intended for exactly this situation.
