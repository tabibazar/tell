# Architectural Design Guidelines for Stan Meadows Townhome Association

<!-- type: architectural; adopted: 2012-02-01; amended: 2019-04-15, 2026-07-01 -->

These Architectural Design Guidelines explain how the Architectural Review Committee applies Article 7 of the Declaration. They cover the application process, review timelines, what is pre-approved, and the approved colour options for the items an Owner may choose. They do not replace the Declaration or the Rules and Regulations, which still govern whether something needs approval at all.

## Article 1 - The Review Process

### 1.1 Submitting a request

An Owner submits an architectural request through the Summit resident app or in writing to management, describing the change, its location on the Lot, materials, dimensions, and, where relevant, colour. Photographs or a simple sketch are encouraged for anything other than a pre-approved item. An incomplete request is returned with a note of what is missing before the 30-day review clock starts.

### 1.2 Review timeline

The Architectural Review Committee meets monthly, on the second Tuesday, by video conference, and acts on complete requests received before that meeting or carried over from the one before. The Committee must respond in writing within 30 days of a complete submission. If no response is given within 30 days, the request is deemed approved exactly as submitted, and the Owner may proceed.

### 1.3 Appeal of a denial

An Owner whose request is denied may appeal to the full Board by written request within 30 days of the denial, submitting any additional information addressing the Committee's stated reason. The Board's decision on appeal is final within the Association's internal process, without prejudice to any right the Owner has under law.

## Article 2 - Pre-Approved Items

### 2.1 Items that do not require a full application

The following may proceed without a full architectural application, though some still require a brief notice to the Committee: a satellite dish under one meter across, mounted preferably away from street-facing walls; a solar energy installation, for which the Owner must still submit a placement notice so the Committee can record it, though it cannot be refused on appearance; an indoor EV charger installed entirely within the garage; drought-tolerant landscaping within existing beds, which instead receives fast-track review rather than no review; and routine plant replacement that does not remove a tree over 6 inches in trunk diameter.

### 2.2 Items that always require full review

A shed, a privacy screen or fence variance, a deck or patio enclosure, a pool or hot tub, an outdoor EV charging unit, a roof material change, replacement windows or doors that differ from the original style, and removal of a tree over 6 inches across all require a complete application and Committee action, even where the change seems minor to the Owner.

## Article 3 - Colour, Materials and Dimensions

### 3.1 Siding colour schemes

Exterior siding and trim are maintained by the Association and are not an Owner choice. The community is finished in three Board-adopted siding schemes, assigned by building so that neighboring buildings read as a coordinated streetscape rather than a single repeated colour: Harbor Mist (a soft blue-grey body with white trim), Cedar Grove (a warm taupe body with dark bronze trim), and Sandstone Row (a light sandstone body with charcoal trim). An Owner may confirm their building's assigned scheme with management; it does not change when the Association repaints on its maintenance cycle.

### 3.2 Front door colour palette

An Owner may choose their own front entry door colour from six Board-approved options, selected to complement all three siding schemes: Classic White, Harbor Blue, Cedar Red, Charcoal Black, Sage Green, and Sandstone Tan. A door painted outside this list, or in a custom mixed shade represented as one of these names, is treated as an unapproved architectural change. An Owner who believes their existing door colour is a reasonable match to a listed option, rather than a different colour entirely, may ask the Committee for a side-by-side comparison against the official paint chip kept with management before a violation notice is issued.

### 3.3 Sheds

A shed may be no larger than 120 square feet in footprint and no taller than 10 feet at its highest point, must sit behind the home out of view from the street where possible, and must be finished in the same siding scheme assigned to the Owner's building under Section 3.1, including matching trim colour. A shed finished in a different scheme's colours, or in an off-palette colour, does not meet this standard even if the Owner finds it a close visual match.

### 3.4 Fences

Because front yards are Common Area with no Owner fencing, fence requests at Stan Meadows generally concern a small privacy enclosure around a patio, pool, or hot tub rather than a yard perimeter. Any approved fence must be a style and height the Committee has pre-published as acceptable for the relevant use, finished in a neutral tone that does not clash with the building's siding scheme, and, for a pool or hot tub enclosure, must independently meet the city's pool safety code regardless of architectural approval.

## Article 4 - Setbacks and Heights

### 4.1 Rear-yard improvement limits

A shed, patio enclosure, or other rear-yard structure must be set back at least 3 feet from the rear property line and 5 feet from a side property line, and may not exceed 10 feet in height, to preserve sightlines for neighboring Lots and for the Association's maintenance access described in the Declaration's utility easement.

### 4.2 Flagpoles and antennas

A flagpole for a flag other than the national, state, or military flags described in the Rules and Regulations may be no taller than the roofline of the home. A freestanding antenna or mast, other than a permitted satellite dish, requires full architectural review under Article 2.2.

## Article 5 - Maintenance of Approved Improvements

### 5.1 Upkeep obligation

Once the Committee approves an improvement, the Owner must keep it in good repair and in its approved colour and condition. A previously approved shed or enclosure that becomes visibly deteriorated, or that the Owner repaints outside the palette rules in Article 3, is subject to a new violation notice even though the original installation was approved.
