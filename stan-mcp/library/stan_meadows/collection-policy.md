# Collection Policy of Stan Meadows Townhome Association

<!-- type: collection; adopted: 2012-02-01; amended: 2019-04-15 -->

This policy explains how Stan Meadows Townhome Association bills, collects, and, where necessary, pursues unpaid assessments, late fees, and fines, implementing Article 8 of the Declaration. It applies equally to every Lot regardless of whether the Owner occupies it or leases it.

## Article 1 - Assessment Due Dates

### 1.1 Monthly due date

The regular assessment of $340 is due on the 1st of each month. Payment is considered on time if received, or postmarked where mail is used, on or before the 10th of the month. The assessment funds roof and siding reserves, common-area landscaping, the gate and its security systems, the pool, the fitness room, and trash service, among the Association's other common expenses.

### 1.2 Payment methods

An Owner may pay by autopay enrollment, one-time electronic payment, or check through the Summit resident app, or by mailing a check to the address management provides. Autopay is encouraged and, once enrolled, draws the assessment automatically on or before the 1st so the Owner does not need to remember the due date each month.

## Article 2 - Late Fees and Interest

### 2.1 Late fee

An assessment not received by the 10th of the month incurs a late fee of $30, applied automatically to the account on the 11th. The late fee is a flat charge per late month, not a percentage of the balance, so it applies the same whether the assessment is one day late or the full month goes unpaid, and it is charged again each subsequent month the account remains behind.

### 2.2 Interest on overdue balances

In addition to the late fee, an assessment balance unpaid after 30 days accrues interest at the rate the Board sets annually and publishes in the budget materials, applied monthly to the unpaid principal until the balance is paid in full or a payment plan under Article 3 is in place.

## Article 3 - Payment Plans

### 3.1 Availability

An Owner who falls behind may request a payment plan of up to 4 months to bring the account current, by contacting management before the account is referred to collections under Article 4. A payment plan request should be made as soon as the Owner knows they cannot pay on time, since a plan already in default is harder to re-establish.

### 3.2 Plan terms

A payment plan divides the delinquent balance, including any late fees already assessed, into equal installments added to the regular monthly assessment for the plan's duration. Interest under Section 2.2 is suspended while the Owner is current on an active plan, but resumes if the Owner misses an installment. Management confirms the plan terms in writing before the first installment is due, including the installment amount, the number of months, and what happens under Section 3.3 if a payment is missed.

### 3.3 Default on a plan

Missing two installments under a payment plan ends the plan, and the full remaining balance becomes due immediately, subject to the same 60-day collections referral in Article 4 as if no plan had been made. An Owner whose plan defaults may request a new plan only once in a 12-month period, at the Board's discretion, to prevent the arrangement from being used to indefinitely delay an otherwise overdue balance.

## Article 4 - Delinquency and Collections

### 4.1 Referral to the attorney

An account more than 60 days past due, including any Owner on a defaulted payment plan, is referred to the Association's collections attorney. Once referred, further payment arrangements go through the attorney rather than management, and the Owner is responsible for the Association's reasonable attorney's fees and collection costs in addition to the underlying balance.

### 4.2 Notice before referral

Before referring an account, management sends a final notice at 45 days past due, stating the balance, the 60-day referral deadline, and the availability of a payment plan under Article 3 if contacted before that deadline. The final notice is sent to the mailing address and email on file and is also posted to the Owner's account in the Summit resident app, giving the Owner roughly two weeks to act before the account leaves management's hands.

## Article 5 - Liens and Foreclosure

### 5.1 Recording a lien

Once an account is referred for collection, the Association's attorney may record a lien against the Lot for the unpaid assessments, late fees, interest, fines, and collection costs, as authorized by Article 8 of the Declaration. The lien remains of record until the balance, including costs of recording and releasing it, is paid in full.

### 5.2 Foreclosure as a last resort

If a lien remains unpaid, the Association may pursue foreclosure of the lien as allowed by state law. The Board treats foreclosure as a last resort after a payment plan, attorney collection effort, and reasonable opportunity to resolve the balance have failed, given the serious consequence to the Owner.

## Article 6 - Returned Payments and Suspension of Privileges

### 6.1 Returned payments

A check or electronic payment returned for insufficient funds incurs a $30 returned-payment fee in addition to any late fee that results from the payment not clearing by the due date, and the Owner may be required to pay by certified funds for a period afterward, typically six months, before being allowed to return to a personal check or standard electronic payment.

### 6.2 Suspension of amenity access

An account more than 60 days delinquent may have its pool, fitness room, dog park, and guest suite access suspended until the balance is brought current or a payment plan is in place, consistent with Section 3.1 of the Declaration. Suspension is administrative, carried out by deactivating the household's amenity access in the gate and fob system, and is lifted automatically once management confirms the account is current again.

## Article 7 - Application of Payments

### 7.1 Order of application

A payment received on a delinquent account is applied first to outstanding collection costs and attorney's fees, then to fines, then to interest, then to late fees, and finally to the oldest unpaid assessment, regardless of any instruction the Owner writes on the payment, unless the Board agrees in writing to a different order as part of a payment plan.
