# Fine and Enforcement Policy of Stan Meadows Townhome Association

<!-- type: enforcement; adopted: 2012-02-01; amended: 2019-04-15, 2026-07-01 -->

This policy sets out how Stan Meadows Townhome Association investigates and penalizes violations of the Declaration, the Rules and Regulations, and the Architectural Guidelines, consistent with Article 10 of the Declaration. It adopts the fine schedule the Board approved and describes the notice, hearing, and appeal rights an Owner has before a fine becomes final.

## Article 1 - General Approach

### 1.1 Purpose of enforcement

Enforcement exists to bring a Lot back into compliance, not to raise revenue for the Association. Management and the Board favor a courtesy notice and cure period over a fine wherever the rule allows it, and escalate only when a first notice does not resolve the issue or where a rule, such as the short-term rental ban, calls for an immediate fine.

### 1.2 Who may report a violation

A violation may be identified by the managing agent, a Board or committee member, or a vendor performing work on the property, or may be reported by another Owner. An anonymous complaint is investigated like any other, but the Association does not issue a notice based on an unverified complaint alone; management confirms the condition before a notice is sent.

## Article 2 - Notice and Cure Process

### 2.1 Courtesy notice

For most first-time violations, management sends a courtesy notice at no charge, describing the violation, the rule it breaks, and the cure period, which is 14 days from the date of the notice. No fine attaches to a courtesy notice, and the matter is closed once management confirms the condition is corrected within the cure period. For example, a charcoal grill found operating or stored within 10 feet of the siding results in a courtesy notice with 14 days to remove it, as this is treated the same as any other first-time rule violation.

### 2.2 Second notice

If the same violation is not cured within the courtesy period, or recurs, management issues a second notice carrying a $50 fine and a further 14-day cure period. The fine is billed to the Owner's account and is due on the same terms as an assessment under the Collection Policy. The second notice also tells the Owner, for the first time, that a further uncured recurrence can lead to a hearing and the continuing fine described in Article 3, so the Owner has a clear sense of what is at stake before matters escalate.

### 2.3 Some violations skip the courtesy stage

Certain violations are serious enough that the fine schedule in Article 4 applies on the first notice rather than after a courtesy period: an unapproved architectural change, a short-term rental, and a pool or clubhouse rule violation. These are addressed in Article 4 rather than through the two-notice process in this Article.

## Article 3 - Hearings and Continuing Violations

### 3.1 Right to a hearing

An Owner who receives a second notice, or a notice under Article 4, may request a hearing before the Board within 14 days of the notice by writing to management. At the hearing, held at a regular or special Board meeting or in executive session, the Owner may present evidence and argument before the Board decides whether the violation occurred and whether the fine stands.

### 3.2 Continuing violation fine

If a violation continues after a hearing confirms it, the Board may impose a continuing fine of $25 per day until the violation is cured, capped at $1,000 total for that violation. The daily fine stops accruing once management confirms the cure, even if the confirmation happens mid-cycle. Reaching the $1,000 cap does not excuse the Owner from curing the violation; the Board may instead pursue the injunctive remedy described in Article 10 of the Declaration once the cap is reached and the condition still has not been corrected.

### 3.3 Hearing procedure

A hearing is informal: the Owner may speak, present photographs or documents, and bring a witness. The Board deliberates and issues its decision in writing within 10 days of the hearing, including the amount of any fine and the deadline, if any, remaining to cure the violation. An Owner who cannot attend the scheduled hearing may ask management, before the hearing date, to reschedule to the next regular meeting, though a repeated request to reschedule may be denied if it appears intended only to delay.

## Article 4 - Specific Fine Schedule

### 4.1 Unapproved architectural change

An exterior change made without required architectural approval, or that does not match what was approved, carries a fine of $250 and requires the Owner to restore the Lot to its approved or original condition within 30 days, in addition to the fine. The fine and the restoration obligation are independent; paying the fine does not excuse leaving the unapproved change in place.

### 4.2 Short-term rental

Renting a home for fewer than 30 days results in a fine of $500 for that stay, due immediately on notice, without a cure period, because the violation is complete once the stay occurs. A repeat short-term rental results in a further $500 fine for each additional stay and may be referred to the Board for consideration of further legal remedies.

### 4.3 Pool or clubhouse rule violation

A violation of the pool or fitness room rules, such as bringing more than four guests, smoking in an enclosed amenity space, or allowing an unaccompanied minor under 16 in the fitness room, carries a fine of $100 and may result in loss of amenity access for up to 30 days, at the Board's discretion based on the severity and whether it is a repeat violation.

## Article 5 - Appeals

### 5.1 Appeal of a fine

An Owner may appeal any fine under this policy to the full Board within 30 days of the fine being issued or of a hearing decision under Article 3, by written request to management stating the grounds for appeal. The Board may uphold, reduce, or waive the fine. Filing an appeal does not stop a continuing fine from accruing under Section 3.2 unless the Board grants a stay.

## Article 6 - Payment and Records

### 6.1 Billing and payment of fines

A fine under this policy is billed to the Owner's assessment account and is due within 30 days of the notice becoming final, meaning after any cure period expires uncured or any hearing or appeal concludes. An unpaid fine is treated as a delinquent assessment under the Collection Policy, including the possibility of a lien under Article 8 of the Declaration.

### 6.2 Violation records

Management keeps a record of every notice and fine issued against a Lot, identified by violation number, for at least three years, and makes it available to the Owner of that Lot on request and to a prospective buyer as part of the resale disclosure described in the Declaration. A violation marked cured and closed in management's records is disclosed as resolved, with its history available, rather than as an open or pending matter, so a buyer sees an accurate current status rather than old history presented as unresolved.
