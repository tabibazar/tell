# Rules and Regulations of Stan Meadows Townhome Association

<!-- type: rules; adopted: 2011-08-01; amended: 2019-04-15, 2026-07-01 -->

These Rules and Regulations implement the use restrictions in the Declaration and apply to every Owner, tenant, household member, and guest at Stan Meadows. The Board last updated them in July 2026. A violation of any rule in this document is enforced under the Fine and Enforcement Policy.

## Part 1 - Vehicles and Parking

### 1.1 Resident and visitor parking

Each townhome has a two-car garage, and residents must park their own vehicles in it rather than in a visitor space overnight. Visitor spaces are marked and reserved for guests holding a one-time or multi-day guest pass issued through the Summit resident app; a visitor pass is valid for up to three nights in a row. Full gate-credential and towing procedures are in the Gate and Parking Policy.

### 1.2 Moving trucks

Moving trucks and large delivery vehicles may use the community streets between 8 am and 8 pm. An Owner planning a move should notify management at least one week ahead so a street can be kept clear for the truck and so neighbors are not blocked in. Management coordinates with the gate vendor to arrange temporary access for the moving company and may ask the Owner to designate a specific visitor space or stretch of curb for the truck to use, particularly on a narrower street.

## Part 2 - Pets and Animals

### 2.1 Household pets

A household may keep up to three pets. Dogs must be leashed whenever they are in a Common Area, and the handler must pick up and dispose of waste immediately. A dog that continues barking after the Owner has received two written warnings is treated as a nuisance violation under the Fine and Enforcement Policy. Rules specific to the dog park, including licensing and vaccination proof, are in the Amenities and Guest Suite Policy.

## Part 3 - Trash, Recycling and Curb Appearance

### 3.1 Bin placement and collection

Trash and recycling bins may be set out after 6 pm the night before the Thursday collection and must be returned out of sight by 8 pm on collection day itself. Bins left out longer, or stored in view from the street on non-collection days, are a curb-appearance violation.

## Part 4 - Exterior Appearance

### 4.1 Paint, siding and door colour

Exterior siding and paint are maintained by the Association as a common expense and may not be altered by an Owner. An Owner may choose their front entry door colour from six Board-approved options; the full palette, named by colour, is in the Architectural Guidelines. No other exterior surface may be repainted or refinished independently.

### 4.2 Sheds

A Lot may have one shed, no larger than 120 square feet and no taller than 10 feet, placed behind the home and finished to match the home's assigned siding colour scheme. A shed requires architectural approval before it is built or placed. The setback and trim-matching detail an Owner needs before submitting a shed request is in the Architectural Guidelines, including which of the three community siding schemes applies to their building.

### 4.3 Satellite dishes and solar panels

A satellite dish under one meter across is permitted wherever needed for reception, preferably mounted so it does not face the street. Solar panels are permitted; state law protects an Owner's right to install them, but the Owner must still submit a placement request so the Architectural Review Committee can note where the panels will sit. Neither may be refused on looks alone.

### 4.4 EV charging equipment

A home EV charger installed entirely inside the garage needs no architectural approval. An outdoor charging unit, including any mounted on an exterior wall or near the driveway apron, requires approval of its placement before installation, since an exterior unit affects the siding and the driveway apron the Owner maintains under the Declaration. Submitting the manufacturer's spec sheet with the request speeds up the Committee's review of an outdoor unit.

### 4.5 Fences

No Lot may have a fence enclosing its front yard, because front yards at Stan Meadows are Common Area maintained by the Association rather than space an Owner fences off individually. A fence is permitted only as a small rear-yard enclosure around a patio, pool, or hot tub, built to the style and height the Architectural Review Committee has pre-published as acceptable and requiring approval before it goes up. A fence shared along a boundary between two Lots, where one has been approved, is maintained equally by both neighbors under the Declaration, though the Association still enforces its style.

### 4.6 Landscaping and trees

Front yards are Common Area maintained by the Association's landscaping contractor, Evergreen Grounds, and Owners should direct front-yard requests to management rather than doing the work themselves. On any Lot, grass must be kept under 6 inches and beds kept weeded. Removing a tree with a trunk over 6 inches across requires architectural approval, whether on the front yard or elsewhere on the Lot. Drought-tolerant landscaping proposals receive fast-track review by the Architectural Review Committee.

## Part 5 - Patios, Grills and Private Pools

### 5.1 Back patios and grills

A back patio belongs to the Owner to keep tidy; outdoor furniture and grills are allowed, but the patio may not be used to store unused items or junk. Gas and electric grills are permitted on back patios only. A charcoal grill may never be used within 10 feet of the building, because of the fire and siding-damage risk; a charcoal grill against or near the siding is a violation even if the Owner intended to move it before lighting it.

### 5.2 Private pools and hot tubs

A private pool or hot tub requires architectural approval before installation and must be enclosed by a fence meeting the city's pool safety code. This is in addition to, not instead of, any fence-style approval that would otherwise apply. An Owner should bring the city's permit and inspection requirements to the Architectural Review Committee alongside the Association's own application, since the Committee's approval does not substitute for city permitting and both are required before work begins.

## Part 6 - Noise and Nuisance

### 6.1 Quiet hours and construction noise

Quiet hours run from 10 pm to 7 am, during which unreasonable noise audible outside a home is a violation. Construction and lawn equipment may be used from 8 am to 6 pm on weekdays and 9 am to 5 pm on Saturdays, and may not be used at all on Sundays. This includes an Owner's own lawn mower or power tools as well as a contractor's equipment, and a contractor working on an Association capital project, such as the Building 7 siding repair, follows the same weekday and Saturday hours.

### 6.2 Smoking and drones

Smoking is prohibited in the clubhouse, the pool area, and any other enclosed Common Area space. A drone may not be flown over another home or over the pool while anyone is using it, whether operated by a resident or a guest. An Owner who wants aerial photographs of their own Lot, for a sale listing or otherwise, should fly only directly over their own property and avoid the pool and neighboring Lots entirely.

## Part 7 - Signs, Flags and Decorations

### 7.1 Signs

An Owner may display one "for sale" sign of up to 6 square feet. Political signs may go up no more than 45 days before an election and must come down within 7 days after it. A security-company sign may be placed near the front door at any time. No sign of any kind may be placed in the Common Area front yard beyond the small footprint directly at the entry, since the front yard itself belongs to the Association, not the Owner.

### 7.2 Flags

The United States flag, the state flag, and flags representing a branch of the military may be flown at any time, consistent with state law protecting a homeowner's right to display them. Any other flag, including a seasonal, sports, or decorative flag, must be flown from a pole no taller than the roofline of the home, and a bracket-mounted flag should be placed so it does not obstruct a shared walkway or sightline.

### 7.3 Holiday decorations

Holiday decorations may go up no more than 30 days before the holiday and must come down within 15 days after it, regardless of which holiday is being observed. This applies to lights, inflatables, wreaths, and seasonal displays on the home's exterior or in the small entry area; décor left up well past the 15-day window is treated the same as any other curb-appearance violation under the Fine and Enforcement Policy.

## Part 8 - Leasing and Home Occupations

### 8.1 Rental terms

A lease shorter than 30 days is a short-term rental and is not permitted at Stan Meadows under any circumstance, including a listing on a short-term rental platform for even a single night. A lease of 30 days or longer must be registered with management, with a copy of the signed lease, within 10 days of signing, and the Owner should provide the tenant a copy of these Rules and Regulations at move-in.

### 8.2 Home-based businesses

A home office is permitted inside the home, provided it displays no exterior sign, receives no more than two customer or client visits a day, and has no employee other than a household member working on site. A business that outgrows these limits must relocate off the property. A purely remote occupation that generates no visitors or signage, such as working from home for an outside employer, is not a "home business" for purposes of this rule and needs no notice to the Association.

## Part 9 - Community Events

### 9.1 Garage sales

The Association hosts two community-wide garage sale weekends each year, in May and in September, advertised to the surrounding neighborhood and coordinated with the gate for extra visitor traffic. An individual Owner wishing to hold a garage sale outside those weekends must first obtain Board permission, which is granted or denied based mainly on expected traffic and parking impact.

## Part 10 - Architectural Review

### 10.1 When approval is required

Any change to the outside of a home requires architectural approval before work begins, including a change of colour, a fence, a deck, a patio enclosure, a roof material change, replacement windows, solar equipment, a shed, and any landscaping change beyond planting within existing beds. The Architectural Review Committee responds within 30 days of a complete submission; if it does not respond within 30 days, the request is treated as approved. The full submission procedure and the approved colour palette are in the Architectural Guidelines.

## Part 11 - Gate and Common Area Conduct

### 11.1 Gate codes and guest access

Each household's gate code changes every January 1. An Owner should give a visiting guest a one-time or limited-use pass generated in the Summit resident app rather than sharing their personal code, both for security and so the Association can track who is on the property. Full gate procedures, including contractor access and towing, are in the Gate and Parking Policy.
