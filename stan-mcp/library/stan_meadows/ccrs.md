# Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Stan Meadows Townhome Association

<!-- type: ccrs; adopted: 2011-06-01; amended: 2019-04-15 -->

This Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions ("Declaration") is recorded against all ninety-six (96) townhome Lots in Stan Meadows, a gated community of sixteen (16) buildings in Cedar Falls, established in 2011. It runs with the land and binds every present and future Owner. It was last amended on April 15, 2019. Where this Declaration and the Association's other governing documents differ, this Declaration controls.

## Article 1 - Definitions

### 1.1 Association and Board

The "Association" means Stan Meadows Townhome Association, a nonprofit corporation whose members are the Owners of the 96 Lots. The "Board" means its Board of Directors, elected under the Bylaws. The Association acts through its Board and, for day-to-day administration, through its managing agent, currently Summit HOA Services. Nothing in this Declaration gives an individual Owner the authority to act for the Association; only the Board, or management acting on the Board's instruction, may do so.

### 1.2 Lot and Unit

A "Lot" means one of the 96 townhome units shown on the recorded plat, together with the building improvements standing on it, identified by a unit number and building number (for example, Unit 14B, Building 4). "Owner" means the person or persons holding recorded title to a Lot, whether or not they occupy it. A Lot held by more than one person counts as a single Lot for voting and assessment purposes.

### 1.3 Common Area

"Common Area" means all real property and improvements owned or maintained by the Association for the common use of Owners, including the entry gate and access road, the perimeter wall and fencing, the pool, the fitness room, the dog park, the guest suite, the cluster mailboxes, the street trees and landscaped strips between sidewalk and street, and the front yards of every Lot, which are Common Area maintained by the Association even though they sit in front of an individually owned Lot.

### 1.4 Governing Documents

"Governing Documents" means this Declaration, the Articles of Incorporation, the Bylaws, the Rules and Regulations, the Architectural Guidelines, and any policy resolution the Board adopts under them, including the Fine and Enforcement Policy, the Collection Policy, and the Gate and Parking Policy. Every Owner is presumed to know the Governing Documents and agrees, by accepting a deed to a Lot, to comply with them.

## Article 2 - The Property

### 2.1 Description and plan of development

Stan Meadows consists of 96 two-storey townhome Lots arranged in 16 buildings on a single parcel in Cedar Falls, built in 2011 and enclosed by a perimeter wall with one controlled vehicle gate. The community was developed as a single phase; no additional Lots may be annexed to the Association without an amendment to this Declaration approved as set out in Article 11.

### 2.2 Gated access

The entire community is gated. The Association owns and operates the entry gate and its access-control system under contract with SecureEntry Systems, and every Owner's membership includes the right to a gate credential for themselves, their household, and their guests, subject to the Gate and Parking Policy. The gate is Common Area; no Owner may alter it, prop it open for extended periods, or share their personal access code as a substitute for a registered guest pass.

## Article 3 - Common Area and Easements

### 3.1 Owners' easement of enjoyment

Every Owner and their household members, tenants, and guests have a non-exclusive easement of use and enjoyment over the Common Area, including the pool, fitness room, dog park, and guest suite, subject to the Rules and Regulations and any amenity hours the Board sets. The Association may suspend an Owner's amenity access for unpaid assessments over sixty (60) days delinquent or for amenity-rule violations, as provided in the Fine and Enforcement Policy.

### 3.2 Utility and maintenance easements

The Association reserves an easement over each Lot's exterior surfaces, front yard, and any shared wall for the installation, inspection, and maintenance of roofing, siding, gutters, irrigation, cable and utility lines, and the gate access system. Vendors acting for the Association, including Evergreen Grounds, Peak Roofing, and SecureEntry Systems, may enter a front yard or exterior area under this easement on reasonable notice, or without notice in an emergency.

### 3.3 Encroachments

If any part of the Common Area encroaches on a Lot, or part of a Lot's building encroaches on the Common Area or an adjoining Lot, because of settling, minor construction variance, or repair work performed under the Governing Documents, a valid easement for the encroachment exists for as long as the encroaching improvement stands.

## Article 4 - Membership and Voting Rights

### 4.1 Membership

Every Owner is automatically a member of the Association. Membership cannot be separated from ownership of a Lot and transfers automatically with the deed. There is no other class of membership and no membership fee beyond the regular and special assessments set out in Article 8. Membership ends automatically when an Owner's title is conveyed to a new party, and begins for the new Owner on the date the deed is recorded, without any application or approval by the Association. A tenant leasing a Lot is not a member and has no vote, even though the Rules and Regulations bind the tenant's conduct on the property.

### 4.2 Voting

Each Lot carries one vote, regardless of how many persons hold title to it or whether it is owner-occupied or leased. Where a Lot has more than one Owner, they must agree among themselves how their single vote is cast; the Association is not required to resolve a dispute between co-Owners over a vote. Voting procedures, proxies, and quorum are set out in the Bylaws.

## Article 5 - Maintenance Responsibilities

### 5.1 Association-maintained building components

The Association maintains, repairs, and replaces, at its expense through the regular assessment: the roof of every building; exterior siding and paint; the front yard landscaping of every Lot; street lights; street trees in the strip between sidewalk and street; the cluster mailboxes (the Association owns them; the Postal Service controls keys and locks); common-area landscaping generally, through its contractor Evergreen Grounds; and pest control in Common Areas. The Association also repairs trip hazards in the public sidewalk in front of each home, though clearing snow and leaves from that sidewalk is the Owner's job under Section 5.2.

### 5.2 Owner-maintained components

Each Owner maintains, at their own expense: the interior of their home, including water heater, furnace, and interior plumbing; windows and doors; the back patio and anything on it; the driveway apron serving their garage; any tree growing on their Lot, including the cost of removing one that falls onto Common Area; and pest control inside their home. An Owner must clear snow and leaves from the public sidewalk directly in front of their Lot.

### 5.3 Shared and boundary improvements

A fence built between two Lots, where one exists under an approved variance, is maintained equally by the two neighboring Owners; the Association enforces only its architectural style, not its repair. A fence along a Common Area is maintained by the Association once it has been installed with architectural approval, or by the Owner who built it without approval. The city utility, not the Association, is responsible for the water main and sewer line within the street, and for storm drains; Owners should report a blocked storm drain to management, which will refer it to the city.

### 5.4 Failure to maintain

If an Owner fails to maintain a component assigned to them under Section 5.2 and the condition threatens the Common Area, a neighboring Lot, or the community's appearance, the Association may give notice and, after the cure period in the Fine and Enforcement Policy, perform the work itself and assess its cost, plus a reasonable administrative charge, against the Lot as a special individual assessment collectible under Article 8.

## Article 6 - Use Restrictions

### 6.1 Residential use and leasing

Every Lot is restricted to residential use. An Owner may lease their home, but no lease may run for a term shorter than 30 days; short-term or vacation rentals are prohibited outright. An Owner who signs a lease of 30 days or longer must register it with management, providing a copy of the lease, within 10 days of signing. The Owner remains responsible for their tenant's compliance with every Governing Document.

### 6.2 Home occupations

An Owner may operate a home-based office or business inside their home if it generates no exterior sign, no more than two customer or client visits to the home per day, and no employee who is not a household member working on site. A home occupation that grows beyond these limits must stop or relocate off the property; this is treated as a use violation under the Fine and Enforcement Policy.

### 6.3 Nuisances and pets

No Owner may maintain a nuisance, including a condition or activity that unreasonably disturbs neighbors. A household may keep up to three pets. Dogs must be leashed in Common Areas and waste picked up immediately; a dog that keeps barking after two written warnings is a nuisance violation. Detailed pet, noise, and amenity rules appear in the Rules and Regulations and the amenity policies.

### 6.4 Signs, flags and exterior changes

Exterior signage is limited to one "for sale" sign of up to 6 square feet, political signs within the windows set by the Rules and Regulations, and a security-company sign near the entry door. Flags are addressed in the Rules and Regulations. No Owner may alter the exterior appearance of their Lot, including paint, fencing, decks, patios, roofing, windows, solar equipment, or landscaping beyond plantings, without architectural approval as required by Article 7.

## Article 7 - Architectural Control

### 7.1 Architectural Review Committee

The Board appoints an Architectural Review Committee, currently meeting monthly on the second Tuesday by video conference, to review requests for exterior changes under this Article and the Architectural Guidelines. No Owner may begin exterior work requiring approval before receiving it, except as the Architectural Guidelines identify as pre-approved. Committee members serve at the Board's pleasure and need not themselves be directors, though the Board may appoint one of its own members to sit on the Committee to keep it informed of broader Association business, such as a pending capital project that might affect the style the Committee is willing to approve nearby.

### 7.2 Review standard and deadline

The Committee reviews each request against the Architectural Guidelines then in effect and against the character of the community. The Committee must respond in writing within 30 days of receiving a complete request; if it does not respond within 30 days, the request is deemed approved as submitted. A denial must state the reason and, where practical, what change would make the request acceptable.

### 7.3 Protected improvements

Consistent with state law, the Committee may not refuse a request to install a solar energy device solely on aesthetic grounds, though the Owner must still submit a request so the Committee can note the device's placement. Satellite dishes under one meter are permitted without a full architectural application, subject to the placement guidance in the Rules and Regulations.

### 7.4 Unapproved work

Exterior work performed without required approval, or that does not match what was approved, is a violation subject to the fine set out in the Fine and Enforcement Policy and may be ordered removed or restored to its approved or original condition at the Owner's expense, regardless of whether the fine has been paid.

## Article 8 - Assessments and Liens

### 8.1 Obligation to pay assessments

Every Owner, by accepting title to a Lot, agrees to pay the Association's regular monthly assessment and any special assessment properly adopted, together with late fees, interest, and collection costs as set out in the Collection Policy. The regular assessment funds roof and siding reserves, landscaping, the gate and its security systems, the pool, the fitness room, trash service, insurance, management, and the Association's other common expenses, as itemized in the annual budget.

### 8.2 Special assessments

The Board may levy a special assessment for a capital expense or reserve shortfall not covered by the regular assessment or reserve fund, following the procedure in the Bylaws for Board action and, where the amount requires it, membership approval. Special assessments are billed per Lot in equal shares, since each Lot carries one assessment obligation regardless of size variation within the building types.

### 8.3 Assessment lien

The Association has a lien on each Lot for unpaid assessments, late fees, interest, fines properly levied under the Fine and Enforcement Policy, and the Association's costs of collection, including reasonable attorney's fees, from the date the amount becomes due. The lien is recorded and enforced as described in the Collection Policy and survives a sale of the Lot to the extent allowed by law.

### 8.4 Statement of account

Upon request, management will provide a prospective buyer, lender, or title company a written statement of a Lot's assessment status, including any balance due, pending violation, or special assessment, for a reasonable fee set by the Board. An Owner should expect several business days for this statement during a sale.

## Article 9 - Insurance

### 9.1 Master policy

The Association maintains a master insurance policy covering the building exteriors, roofs, and Common Area, with a $10,000 deductible payable from the Association's operating funds or reserves when a covered loss occurs. The master policy does not cover an Owner's interior finishes, personal property, or liability within their own home. If a covered loss affects more than one building, such as storm damage to several roofs at once, the Board decides whether to fund the deductible from operating funds, the reserve fund, or a special assessment, depending on the size of the loss and the state of the reserve fund at the time.

### 9.2 Owner's responsibility

Each Owner must carry an HO-6 "walls-in" homeowner's policy covering their interior, personal property, and personal liability, including loss-assessment coverage that would reimburse the Owner's share of a special assessment arising from an insured Common Area loss. The Association may require proof of this coverage at closing or upon reasonable request.

## Article 10 - Enforcement

### 10.1 General enforcement authority

The Board may enforce this Declaration and the other Governing Documents by written notice, fines under the Fine and Enforcement Policy, suspension of amenity privileges, a lawsuit for injunctive relief or damages, or, for unpaid amounts, the lien and collection remedies in Article 8 and the Collection Policy. Enforcement may be pursued against the Owner even where a tenant or guest caused the violation.

### 10.2 No waiver

The Association's failure to enforce a provision on one occasion is not a waiver of its right to enforce it later, against the same Owner or another. An Owner may not claim that an uneven past practice excuses a current violation, though consistent, documented practice may be considered at a hearing.

## Article 11 - Amendments

### 11.1 Amendment procedure

This Declaration may be amended by an affirmative vote of at least two-thirds of the Lots, at a meeting called for that purpose with at least 30 days' notice stating the proposed amendment, or by written consent of the same proportion of Owners. An approved amendment is recorded against the property and binds all Owners from the date of recording.

## Article 12 - General Provisions

### 12.1 Severability and duration

If a court holds any part of this Declaration invalid, the rest remains in force, and the remaining Articles continue to bind every Owner as if the invalid part had never been included. This Declaration runs with the land for an initial term of 20 years from 2011 and renews automatically for successive 10-year terms unless terminated by the amendment vote in Article 11. Termination does not relieve any Owner of an assessment, fine, or lien that accrued before the termination date.

### 12.2 Notice

Any notice this Declaration or another Governing Document requires the Association to give an Owner is effective when mailed or emailed to the address or email on file with management, or delivered through the Summit resident app. Each Owner must keep a current mailing address and email on file, and notify management promptly after any change, since a notice sent to the address of record is effective even if the Owner no longer lives there or has not updated their contact information.
