# Rules and Regulations for Sheppard Center
<!-- type: rules; adopted: 2003-02-10; amended: 2015-04-12, 2026-03-02 -->

These Rules and Regulations implement the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions and govern the everyday life of Sheppard Center's 212 homes: parking, pets, trash, noise, signs, flags, holiday decorations, rentals and more. The Board adopted the most recent update on March 2, 2026. A violation of any rule in this document is enforced under the Association's Fine and Enforcement Policy.

## Article 1 - Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

These Rules apply to every Owner, tenant, household member and guest at Sheppard Center and to every Lot and all Common Area. They are adopted by the Board under the authority of the Declaration and may be amended by the Board without a membership vote, unlike the Declaration itself, though the Board will give Owners reasonable notice of a significant change before it takes effect.

## Article 2 - Parking and Vehicles

### 2.1 Resident and Guest Parking

Household vehicles must be parked in a Lot's garage or driveway. Guests may park on the street for up to 72 hours continuously; a vehicle parked longer than that without rotating is subject to a courtesy notice and, if it continues, a fine under the Fine and Enforcement Policy. Commercial vehicles weighing more than one ton may not be left on any street or driveway overnight, even while making a delivery earlier in the day.

### 2.2 Boats, RVs and Trailers

Boats may be stored only inside a garage; a boat may sit in a driveway for up to 48 hours solely to be loaded or unloaded for a trip. Recreational vehicles and trailers follow the same 48-hour driveway limit for loading and may not be used as overnight or extended parking or storage anywhere on a Lot or the street.

## Article 3 - Pets

### 3.1 Household Pets

A household may keep up to three pets. Dogs must be leashed at all times outside the Owner's own Lot, including on the walking loop, and an owner must immediately pick up and properly dispose of a pet's waste. A dog that barks persistently enough to disturb neighbors is a nuisance; management will issue two written warnings, and barking that continues after the second warning is treated as a violation subject to a fine.

## Article 4 - Trash, Recycling and Yard Waste

### 4.1 Collection Schedule

Household trash is collected Tuesdays. Recycling is collected every other Tuesday on even weeks. Yard waste is collected Mondays from April through November. Bulk item pickup is booked with the city and occurs the first Friday of the month. Street sweeping takes place the second Wednesday of the month, and Owners should move vehicles off the street that morning if possible. In winter, the city plows the streets; the Association clears the clubhouse parking lot and the walking loop's paths once snow exceeds 2 inches.

### 4.2 Bin Placement

Trash and recycling bins may be placed at the curb no earlier than 6 pm the evening before the scheduled pickup day and must be returned out of street view no later than 8 pm on the pickup day itself. A bin left visible from the street outside this window is a violation of this rule, even if the household is away.

## Article 5 - Noise and Construction Hours

### 5.1 Quiet Hours and Permitted Work Hours

Quiet hours run from 10 pm to 7 am every night. Construction work and lawn equipment may be operated between 8 am and 6 pm on weekdays and between 9 am and 5 pm on Saturdays. No construction or lawn equipment noise is permitted on Sundays at all, regardless of the hour, to preserve one quiet day each week for the whole neighborhood.

## Article 6 - Signs and Flags

### 6.1 Signs

An Owner may display one "for sale" or "for rent" sign no larger than 6 square feet. Political signs may be displayed no earlier than 45 days before an election and must come down within 7 days after it. A security company sign may be placed near the home's entry door at any time. No other commercial, decorative or informational sign is permitted in a yard or window facing the street without Committee approval.

### 6.2 Flags

The United States flag, the state flag and flags representing the branches of the military may be flown at any time without restriction, in any number and without prior approval, reflecting the protections these flags receive under state law. Any other flag, including a decorative, seasonal, collegiate or novelty flag, must be flown from a pole no taller than the roofline of the home, and an Owner installing a new freestanding flagpole for this purpose should check with the Committee first, since the pole itself is an exterior structure subject to the Architectural Guidelines even though the flag it carries is not.

## Article 7 - Holiday Decorations

### 7.1 Timing of Holiday Decorations

Holiday decorations may go up no more than 30 days before the holiday they celebrate and must come down within 15 days after it. This applies to lights, inflatables, yard displays and similar seasonal decoration on the exterior of a home or Lot. Decorations left up beyond the 15-day removal window are treated as a rules violation like any other.

## Article 8 - Short-Term and Long-Term Rentals

### 8.1 Rental Terms

Rentals shorter than 30 days are not permitted anywhere in Sheppard Center under any circumstance, including single-night or weekend bookings through a rental platform. A lease of 30 days or longer is allowed but must be registered with management within 10 days of the lease's start, along with a copy of the signed lease identifying the tenant. An Owner who rents their home remains responsible for the tenant's compliance with these Rules.

## Article 9 - Home Occupations

### 9.1 Home-Based Businesses

A homeowner may operate a home office or home-based business from their Lot provided it displays no exterior sign, does not generate more than two client or customer visits to the home per day, and does not involve an employee who is not a household member working on site. A home occupation that creates parking congestion, noise or traffic beyond these limits will be treated as a nuisance.

## Article 10 - Smoking and Drones

### 10.1 Smoking in Common Areas

Smoking, including vaping and the use of electronic cigarettes, is not permitted inside the clubhouse, anywhere in the pool area including the deck and seating areas, or in any other enclosed Common Area space such as a restroom or storage building. This rule protects both the amenities themselves, including furniture and pool-area surfaces, and the comfort and health of other residents using them. Smoking on a private patio or yard remains an Owner's own choice, subject to the general nuisance standard in the Declaration if smoke drifts persistently into a neighbor's yard or windows.

### 10.2 Drones

A drone may not be flown over another Owner's home without that Owner's permission, nor over the pool area while it is in use by others, nor over the clubhouse during an event. This rule exists to protect residents' privacy and safety around the community's amenities, particularly given how closely the homes and the pool deck sit to one another. An Owner may fly a drone over their own Lot, or over Common Area not in active use by others, consistent with any applicable aviation regulations.

## Article 11 - Garage Sales

### 11.1 Community and Individual Sales

The Association holds two community-wide garage sales each year, in May and September, when individual sales do not require separate approval. An Owner who wishes to hold an individual garage sale outside those two weekends must first obtain permission from the Board, which helps the Association manage traffic and signage across the neighborhood.

## Article 12 - Moving and Deliveries

### 12.1 Moving Trucks

Moving trucks may operate only between 8 am and 8 pm. An Owner planning a move should notify management at least a week in advance so the street in front of the home can be kept clear for the truck, which helps avoid conflicts with street sweeping, trash collection and neighbors' parked vehicles.

## Article 13 - Heron Pond and the Walking Loop

### 13.1 Pond and Loop Conduct

No one may swim, boat or fish in Heron Pond at any time, and ice skating on the pond is not permitted in winter. On the 1.2-mile walking loop, dogs must remain leashed and motorized bikes, including electric scooters and mini-bikes, are not allowed; the loop is reserved for walking, jogging and non-motorized bicycles, consistent with its use as a quiet amenity open from dawn to dusk.

## Article 14 - Enforcement of These Rules

### 14.1 How a Rule Violation Is Handled

A violation of any rule in this document begins with a courtesy notice carrying no fine and a 14-day cure period, and escalates to a $50 second notice, and potentially to a hearing before the Board and a continuing daily fine, exactly as described in the Association's Fine and Enforcement Policy. These Rules do not create a separate fine schedule of their own; they describe the conduct expected of Owners, tenants and guests, while the Fine and Enforcement Policy describes what happens when that conduct is not followed. An Owner who is unsure whether a planned activity, such as an individual garage sale or an outdoor EV charger, requires advance notice or approval should check with management before proceeding, since asking in advance is simpler than curing a notice afterward.

## Article 15 - Contacting Management and Emergency Services

### 15.1 Reaching Management

Day-to-day questions, architectural applications, maintenance reports and account questions go to Lakeside Property Management, the Association's management company, at 555-0142 or sheppardcenter@lakeside.example. The management office is open weekdays from 9 am to 5 pm, and a staff member is also at the clubhouse office on Tuesdays from 4 to 7 pm for Owners who prefer to stop by in person, such as to drop off an architectural application or pick up a pool fob. Owners can also reach management, check their account balance, and submit a request at any hour through the Lakeside owner portal.

### 15.2 After-Hours and Emergency Numbers

For a true emergency, such as a fire or a medical emergency, call 911 first. For a non-emergency matter involving the police, call 555-0111. An urgent problem in the Common Area outside office hours, such as a burst pipe or a fallen tree blocking a path, should go to the after-hours management line at 555-0199. A gas leak should be reported directly to the gas utility at 555-0123, and a power outage to the electric utility at 555-0124; these utility problems are not handled by the Association or its management company, even though management appreciates being kept aware of them when they affect the community.
