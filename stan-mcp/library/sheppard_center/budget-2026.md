# 2026 Budget and Reserve Study Summary for Sheppard Center
<!-- type: budget; adopted: 2025-11-20 -->

The Board adopted the 2026 annual budget on November 20, 2025, following its presentation by Treasurer Anita Brooks. This summary sets out the 2026 operating budget, the reserve fund status, and the major projects identified in the 2024 reserve study prepared by Northgate Reserve Consultants.

## Part 1 - Income

### 1.1 Assessment and Other Income

The 2026 budget is funded primarily by the regular monthly assessment of $285.00 across 212 homes, which produces $725,040.00 in assessment income for the year ($285.00 x 212 homes x 12 months). The remaining $960.00 of budgeted income comes from other sources: pool fob replacement fees, clubhouse rental fees and deposits retained for damage, and interest earned on operating and reserve balances. Total budgeted income for 2026 is $726,000.00, matching total budgeted expenses so that the budget is balanced with no planned draw on reserves beyond the budgeted contribution in Part 2.

### 2.2 Notes on Major Line Items

Landscaping and pond maintenance is the largest single line item, reflecting the contract with GreenEdge Landscapes for the Common Area grounds, the street tree strip, and the ongoing care of Heron Pond. The reserve fund contribution of $120,000.00 is set by the Board each year with reference to the most recent reserve study, described in Part 3, to keep the fund's percent-funded level moving in a healthy direction even though it is not yet fully funded. The $400.00 per home special assessment proposed for the clubhouse roof, to be voted on October 22, 2026, is separate from this operating budget and does not appear in the table above; if approved, it will be tracked and reported separately.

## Part 3 - Reserve Fund Status

### 3.1 Current Reserve Balance

As of the 2026 budget year, the Association's reserve fund holds approximately $412,000.00, which the 2024 reserve study estimated at about 61% of the fund's ideal fully funded level. The reserve fund exists to pay for the replacement of major Common Area components, such as the clubhouse roof, the pool surface and the walking loop's pavement, without requiring a special assessment every time one of those components wears out; a fund below 100% funded, like Sheppard Center's, means a special assessment becomes more likely when a major project's timing and cost do not line up neatly with the balance on hand, as is the case with the clubhouse roof in 2026.

### 3.2 The 2024 Reserve Study

Northgate Reserve Consultants completed Sheppard Center's most recent reserve study in 2024, with the next full study due in 2027. The study identifies the Association's major Common Area components, estimates each one's remaining useful life and replacement cost, and recommends an annual reserve contribution; the Board's $120,000.00 contribution in the 2026 budget reflects that recommendation. The three major projects the study flags for the coming years are the clubhouse roof in 2026, pool resurfacing in 2027, and repaving of the walking loop's paths in 2029.

### 3.3 Major Projects Timeline

The table below summarizes the reserve study's major upcoming projects.

| Project | Target Year | Funding Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Clubhouse roof replacement | 2026 | Reserve funds plus a proposed $400.00 per home special assessment |
| Pool resurfacing | 2027 | Reserve funds |
| Walking loop path repaving | 2029 | Reserve funds |

The clubhouse roof project is the most immediate, which is why the Board is proposing the special assessment described in Part 2 rather than drawing down the reserve fund enough to put other near-term reserve goals at risk. Bids for the pool resurfacing project are already being gathered ahead of the 2027 target so the Board can refine the reserve study's cost estimate before that project's funding year arrives.

## Part 4 - Looking Ahead to 2027

### 4.1 Proposed 2027 Budget

At its September 24, 2026 meeting, the Board reviewed a draft 2027 budget recommending that the regular monthly assessment rise from $285.00 to $299.00, an increase of $14.00 per home per month, intended to keep pace with rising landscaping, insurance and management costs without reducing the reserve fund contribution. This proposal, together with the separate $400.00 per home special assessment for the clubhouse roof, is scheduled for a membership vote at the October 22, 2026 meeting, with electronic voting opening October 8, 2026. Neither figure is reflected in the 2026 budget tables above, which remain governed by the $285.00 assessment adopted for calendar year 2026.

### 4.2 Why a Special Assessment Rather Than a Larger Reserve Draw

The Board considered funding the full clubhouse roof project from the reserve fund alone, but concluded that doing so would drop the fund's percent-funded level well below its current 61%, leaving too little cushion ahead of the 2027 pool resurfacing project and the 2029 path repaving project identified in the reserve study. Spreading part of the roof's cost across a one-time $400.00 per home special assessment, rather than a larger permanent increase to the monthly assessment, keeps the regular assessment increase modest while still protecting the reserve fund's trajectory toward full funding over time.
