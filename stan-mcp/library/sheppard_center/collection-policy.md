# Assessment Collection Policy for Sheppard Center
<!-- type: collection; adopted: 2010-01-01; amended: 2024-06-01 -->

This Collection Policy explains how and when Sheppard Center's regular and special assessments are billed and paid, what happens when an account falls behind, and how an Owner can arrange a payment plan before the matter reaches collections. It is adopted under Article 6 of the Declaration.

## Part 1 - Due Dates and Billing

### 1.1 Regular Assessment Due Date

The regular monthly assessment, currently $285.00, is due on the 1st of each month, with no grace period before a payment is considered late for purposes of the Owner's own records, though the late fee itself does not apply until after the 15th as described in Section 1.2. Owners are billed through the Lakeside owner portal, where an Owner can view their current balance, full payment history and any open violations or requests tied to their account. The assessment is due in full regardless of whether an Owner has reviewed the portal statement that month, and an Owner who has not received a statement remains responsible for paying on time; the Association encourages every Owner to enroll in autopay for exactly this reason.

### 1.2 Late Fees

An account not paid by the 15th of the month incurs a $25 late fee, added automatically to the balance. The late fee applies once per month the payment is late and does not itself trigger collections on its own; it is simply added to the amount owed until the balance, including the late fee, is paid in full.

## Part 2 - Payment Methods

### 2.1 Autopay and Card Payments

The Association offers free ACH autopay through the owner portal, which an Owner can set up at any time to avoid missing the 1st-of-the-month due date; Owners who are current and on autopay, as most are, are not at risk of a late fee. An Owner who prefers to pay by credit or debit card may do so through the portal, but card payments carry a 2.9% processing fee charged by the payment processor, not the Association.

## Part 3 - Payment Plans

### 3.1 Eligibility and Terms

An Owner with a balance under $2,000 may request a payment plan of up to 6 months, provided the request is made before the account reaches the 90-day collections threshold described in Part 4. A typical plan adds a fixed amount to the regular monthly assessment until the past-due balance, including any late fees, is paid off within the agreed term. An Owner on a payment plan must also keep current on new assessments as they come due during the plan; falling behind on current dues while on a plan can end the plan early and return the account to standard collections.

### 3.2 Requesting a Plan

An Owner requests a payment plan by contacting management, which documents the agreed monthly amount and the number of months in the Owner's file and on the portal. Once a plan is in place, the payment schedule it sets controls over the standard late fee and collections timeline described elsewhere in this policy, so long as the Owner keeps to it.

## Part 4 - Delinquency and Collections

### 4.1 The 90-Day Threshold

If an account remains unpaid 90 days after its due date, without an active payment plan, the Association refers the account to its attorney for formal collection. At that point, the Owner is also responsible for the Association's reasonable attorney's fees and collection costs, which are added to the amount owed. Referral to the attorney is not a final step but the point at which the Association turns the matter over for formal pursuit of the debt.

### 4.2 Liens

Once an account is in formal collections, the Association's attorney may record a lien against the Lot for the unpaid assessments, late fees, interest and collection costs. A lien must be satisfied, typically at closing, before the Lot can be sold or refinanced, which is often how a long-standing delinquency is finally resolved. The Association may pursue foreclosure of the lien as a last resort where the law allows, though it prefers a payment plan or a negotiated resolution wherever one is realistic.

## Part 5 - Special Assessments

### 5.1 Collecting a Special Assessment

A special assessment, such as the $400 per home assessment proposed for the clubhouse roof, is billed separately from the regular monthly dues and is due on the date the Board sets when it levies the assessment. An unpaid special assessment follows the same late fee, 90-day referral and lien process as an unpaid regular assessment, since both are assessments against the Lot under the Declaration.

## Part 6 - Resale Certificates and Estoppel Letters

### 6.1 Ordering a Resale Certificate

An Owner selling their home, or their closing agent, may order a resale certificate, sometimes called an estoppel letter, confirming the Owner's assessment balance, any pending violations, and any special assessment obligations that will carry to the buyer. The fee for a resale certificate is $250, paid when the request is placed. Management typically completes and delivers the certificate within 10 days of the paid request, consistent with typical closing timelines.

## Part 7 - Disputed Charges

### 7.1 Disputing a Charge

An Owner who believes a charge on their account is incorrect, such as a late fee applied despite a timely payment, should contact management promptly with supporting documentation such as a bank confirmation. Management will research the payment history and correct a confirmed error without requiring a formal appeal; a dispute over a fine rather than a billing error follows the appeal process in the Fine and Enforcement Policy instead.

## Part 8 - Statements, Notices and Returned Payments

### 8.1 Account Statements and Delinquency Notices

Management sends a delinquency reminder once an account passes its due date without payment, and a further notice if the account is still unpaid after the 15th and the late fee has been applied. These billing reminders are separate from, and may run alongside, any notice issued for a rules or architectural violation under the Fine and Enforcement Policy; an Owner can have both an open violation notice and a delinquency notice on file at the same time, since they track different obligations.

### 8.2 Returned and Failed Payments

A payment that is returned or fails, such as an ACH autopay draft declined for insufficient funds or an online payment that does not clear, is treated as unpaid as of its original due date, and the usual late fee applies if it is not replaced with a successful payment by the 15th. An Owner whose payment method fails repeatedly should contact management to update their autopay details on the portal, since a pattern of failed payments can otherwise carry an account toward the 90-day collections threshold in Part 4 without the Owner realizing it.
