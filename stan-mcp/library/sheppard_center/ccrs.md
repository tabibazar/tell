# Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Sheppard Center
<!-- type: ccrs; adopted: 1998-06-01; amended: 2015-04-12, 2021-09-08 -->

This Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions ("Declaration") is recorded against the 212 residential Lots and the Common Area known as Sheppard Center, a community of detached single-family homes in Maple Grove built between 1998 and 2004 around a clubhouse, a pool, two tennis courts and the 1.2-mile walking loop encircling Heron Pond. Every Lot is held subject to this Declaration, which runs with the land and binds every Owner, tenant and occupant. The Sheppard Center Homeowners Association (the "Association") administers and enforces it.

## Article 1 - Definitions

### 1.1 Definitions

The following terms, capitalized throughout this Declaration, have the meanings given here. "Association" means the Sheppard Center Homeowners Association, a nonprofit corporation of which every Owner is automatically a member. "Owner" means the record title holder of a Lot, including a contract purchaser, but excluding a mortgage holder. "Lot" means one of the 212 platted single-family residential parcels within the community, identified by lot number (for example, Lot 114). "Common Area" means the clubhouse, pool, tennis courts, playground, walking loop, Heron Pond, cluster mailboxes, private landscaped medians, and other property the Association owns or maintains for the common benefit of all Owners. "Board" means the Board of Directors elected under the Bylaws. "Committee" means the Architectural Review Committee described in Article 4. "Declaration" means this instrument as amended from time to time.

## Article 2 - The Properties

### 2.1 Description of the Community

Sheppard Center consists of 212 detached single-family Lots developed in phases between 1998 and 2004, together with the Common Area described in Section 1.1. The community is not gated; streets are dedicated to and maintained by the City of Maple Grove, except for the clubhouse parking lot and the paths around Heron Pond, which are Association property. No further phases remain to be developed, and this Declaration does not provide for annexation of additional land.

### 2.2 Division Into Lots and Common Area

Each Lot is improved with one single-family dwelling and is held in fee simple by its Owner, subject to the easements, restrictions and assessment obligations of this Declaration. The Common Area is held by the Association for the use and enjoyment of all Owners and their household members and guests, subject to the rules adopted under Article 8 and the Association's separate Rules and Regulations.

## Article 3 - Use Restrictions

### 3.1 Residential Use and Home Occupations

Each Lot shall be used only for single-family residential purposes. An Owner may conduct a home office or similar occupation inside the dwelling provided it generates no exterior sign, no customer or client traffic exceeding two visits per day, and no employee working on site. Any occupation that produces noise, odor, traffic or parking demand noticeable from neighboring Lots is a nuisance under Section 3.5 regardless of whether it otherwise complies with this section.

### 3.2 Leasing and Short-Term Rentals

Owners may lease their homes, but rentals of fewer than 30 days are prohibited outright; Sheppard Center is a residential community, not a transient lodging property. A lease of 30 days or longer must be registered with the management company, together with a copy of the signed lease, within 10 days of its commencement. An Owner remains responsible for a tenant's compliance with this Declaration, the Bylaws and the Rules and Regulations, and for any fines arising from the tenant's conduct.

### 3.3 Pets

An Owner may keep up to three household pets. Dogs must be leashed whenever outside the Owner's Lot, including on the walking loop and in the Common Area, and waste must be picked up immediately. A dog that barks persistently is a nuisance; after two written warnings from management, continued barking is treated as a violation subject to the fine schedule in the Association's Fine and Enforcement Policy.

### 3.4 Parking and Vehicles

Vehicles belonging to an Owner's household must be parked in the garage or driveway of that Owner's Lot. Guests may park on the street for up to 72 hours. Boats, recreational vehicles and trailers may be parked in a driveway for no more than 48 hours, solely for loading or unloading, and may not be stored on the street or driveway beyond that period; boats otherwise must be stored in a garage. Commercial vehicles with a gross weight over one ton may not be parked overnight on any street or driveway.

### 3.5 Nuisances, Noise and Signs

No Owner may engage in any activity that is a nuisance or unreasonably interferes with a neighbor's use and enjoyment of their Lot. Quiet hours run from 10 pm to 7 am. Construction and lawn equipment may be used from 8 am to 6 pm on weekdays and 9 am to 5 pm on Saturdays, and not at all on Sundays. Exterior signs are limited to one "for sale" or "for rent" sign up to 6 square feet, political signs displayed no earlier than 45 days before an election and removed within 7 days after it, and a security company sign near the entry door.

### 3.6 Antennas, Satellite Dishes and Solar Energy

Satellite dishes under one meter in diameter are permitted where needed for reception and should, where reception allows, be placed so they do not face the street. Solar energy devices are permitted; state law protects an Owner's right to install them, and the Committee may not refuse a solar application on aesthetic grounds alone, though an Owner must submit a request under Article 4 so the Committee can record the proposed placement.

## Article 4 - Architectural Control

### 4.1 Approval Required

No Owner may make any change to the exterior of a Lot or dwelling, including a change of exterior paint color, a new or replacement fence, a deck, a patio, a change of roofing material, replacement windows, a solar installation, a shed, or landscaping beyond the planting of flowers and shrubs, without first obtaining written approval from the Committee. This requirement exists to preserve the visual consistency that supports property values across all 212 homes.

### 4.2 The Architectural Review Committee

The Committee reviews all applications for exterior changes and maintains the list of approved exterior colors, fence styles and materials referenced in the Association's Architectural Guidelines. The Committee is appointed by the Board, with members serving at the Board's pleasure, and meets regularly, currently the first Monday of each month, to act on pending applications. It keeps minutes of its decisions that are available to the Board and to the applicant, and reports a summary of its activity to the Board at each regular Board meeting so directors stay informed of architectural trends across the 212 homes, including any recurring disputes over colors or materials that may warrant a future update to the Architectural Guidelines.

### 4.3 Review Procedure and Deemed Approval

An Owner seeking approval must submit a written request describing the proposed change, including samples, plans or specifications as the Committee's form requires. The Committee must respond within 30 days of a complete submission; if it does not respond within that period, the request is deemed approved as submitted. A change made without approval, or that departs materially from what was approved, is a violation subject to the fine for an unapproved architectural change and may be required to be restored to its prior condition at the Owner's expense.

## Article 5 - Maintenance Responsibilities

### 5.1 Owner's Maintenance Obligations

Each Owner is responsible for maintaining their Lot, including the dwelling's roof, driveway, interior systems such as the water heater, furnace and plumbing, and all trees located on the Lot, including liability for a Lot tree that falls onto Common Area or a neighboring Lot. An Owner must also clear snow and fallen leaves from the sidewalk fronting their Lot. If a roof becomes visibly failing, the Association may require the Owner to repair or replace it on a reasonable timeline.

### 5.2 Association's Maintenance Obligations

The Association maintains the Common Area, including the clubhouse, pool, tennis courts, playground, walking loop and the landscaped medians, through its landscaping contractor, GreenEdge Landscapes. The Association repairs trip hazards in sidewalks fronting Lots, maintains street lights (Owners should report outages to management), maintains the street trees planted in the strip between the sidewalk and the street, owns and maintains the cluster mailboxes (though the United States Postal Service controls keys and locks), and is responsible for pest control in the Common Area through its vendor, Shield Pest. Storm drains and the water main and sewer lines within the street are the responsibility of the City of Maple Grove's utility; Owners should report blockages to management, which will refer them to the city.

### 5.3 Shared Fences, Trees and Boundaries

A fence built on the boundary line between two Lots is the shared responsibility of both neighboring Owners for maintenance and repair cost; the Association's role is limited to enforcing that the fence's style and height comply with the Architectural Guidelines. A fence along a Common Area boundary is the Association's responsibility once it has been installed with Committee approval; if an Owner built that fence without going through the Association, the Owner remains responsible for it. Disputes between neighbors over a shared fence's condition should first be addressed between the Owners before being brought to management.

## Article 6 - Assessments and Liens

### 6.1 Regular Assessments

Each Owner shall pay a monthly regular assessment, currently $285.00, due on the 1st of each month. The Board sets the regular assessment annually as part of the budget process described in the Bylaws, subject to the membership notice and vote required for an increase. The regular assessment funds the Association's operating budget, including landscaping, management, amenity operation, insurance and the reserve fund contribution.

### 6.2 Special Assessments

The Board may levy a special assessment for a capital expense not covered by the reserve fund, such as the clubhouse roof replacement, subject to any membership vote required by the Bylaws. A special assessment is billed per Lot, is due on the date the Board sets, and is subject to the same late fee, interest and lien provisions as a regular assessment if unpaid.

### 6.3 Lien for Unpaid Assessments and Collection

An unpaid assessment, together with any late fee, interest and the Association's reasonable costs of collection including attorney's fees, constitutes a continuing lien against the Lot from the date it becomes due. If an account remains delinquent for 90 days, the Association refers it to its attorney for collection, and a lien may be recorded against the Lot. The Association's full collection procedure is set out in its separate Collection Policy.

## Article 7 - Insurance

### 7.1 Master Policy and Owner's Insurance

The Association maintains a master insurance policy covering the Common Area, the clubhouse building and general liability in the amount of $2,000,000. This master policy does not cover the interior of any dwelling, personal property, or liability arising from an individual Lot; each Owner is responsible for insuring their own home, typically through an HO-3 homeowner's policy, and the Association carries no insurance on an Owner's behalf for that purpose.

## Article 8 - Common Area and Amenities

### 8.1 Common Area Ownership and Use

The Common Area, including the clubhouse, pool, tennis courts, playground and walking loop, is owned and operated by the Association for the benefit of all Owners in good standing and their household members and guests. Use of each amenity is subject to the posted hours and rules summarized in the Association's Amenities Policy, and access may be suspended for an Owner whose account is delinquent or who has an unresolved amenity-related violation.

### 8.2 Heron Pond

Heron Pond is a decorative and stormwater amenity within the Common Area bordered by the 1.2-mile walking loop. Swimming, boating and fishing in Heron Pond are prohibited at all times, as is ice skating on the pond in winter, both for safety and to protect the pond's ecology, which the landscape committee monitors with GreenEdge Landscapes.

## Article 9 - Enforcement

### 9.1 Violations and Notice

A violation of this Declaration, the Bylaws, the Rules and Regulations or the Architectural Guidelines is addressed first with a courtesy notice carrying no fine and a 14-day period to cure. If the violation continues or recurs, a second written notice is issued carrying a $50 fine and a further 14 days to cure. The complete notice and cure schedule, including fines specific to architectural, rental and amenity violations, appears in the Association's Fine and Enforcement Policy.

### 9.2 Fines and Hearings

Before a fine beyond the second notice is imposed, or before a continuing fine accrues, the Owner is entitled to a hearing before the Board, at which the Owner may appear, present evidence and explain the circumstances. A violation that continues after a hearing may accrue a fine of $25 per day, capped at $1,000. Fines are enforced as an assessment against the Lot and are collectible in the same manner as unpaid dues.

### 9.3 Legal Remedies

In addition to fines, the Association may pursue injunctive relief or other legal remedies to compel compliance with this Declaration, particularly for violations that are not cured by a fine, such as an unapproved structure that is not removed. The prevailing party in any enforcement action is entitled to recover reasonable attorney's fees and costs as provided by law.

## Article 10 - Amendments

### 10.1 Amendment Procedure and History

This Declaration may be amended by a vote of Owners representing a majority of the Lots, at a meeting called for that purpose with proper notice, or by written consent in lieu of a meeting as the Bylaws permit. This Declaration was originally recorded in 1998 and has since been amended twice, in 2015 and again in 2021, principally to update the architectural review timeline, the leasing restrictions and the solar energy provisions to reflect changes in state law. A recorded amendment binds all Owners whether or not they voted in favor of it.

## Article 11 - Easements

### 11.1 Utility, Drainage and Access Easements

The Association and applicable utility providers hold easements over each Lot as shown on the recorded plat, including easements for drainage, for utility lines serving the community, and for the Association's access to maintain the street trees in the strip between the sidewalk and the street and the cluster mailboxes that serve nearby Lots. An Owner may not build a structure, including a shed or fence, within a platted easement in a way that blocks necessary access, and must restore any landscaping disturbed when the Association or a utility exercises its easement rights, at the party exercising the right's expense where it caused the disturbance.

### 11.2 Common Area Access Easement

Every Owner and their household members and guests hold a non-exclusive easement to use the Common Area, including the clubhouse, pool, tennis courts, playground and walking loop, subject to the Amenities Policy and to suspension for cause as described in Article 9. This easement runs with the Lot and passes automatically to a new Owner at closing; it cannot be sold, leased or transferred separately from the Lot itself.

## Article 12 - Severability and Notices

### 12.1 Severability and Notice to Owners

If a court holds any provision of this Declaration invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions continue in full force, and the invalid provision is read as narrowly as necessary to make it enforceable rather than striking it entirely where that is possible. Any notice this Declaration requires to be given to an Owner is sufficient if sent to the Owner's address of record with management, by mail, by the owner portal, or by email on file, and an Owner is responsible for keeping that contact information current with management.
