# Architectural Design Guidelines for Sheppard Center
<!-- type: architectural; adopted: 2002-05-01; amended: 2021-09-08, 2024-01-15 -->

These Architectural Design Guidelines implement Article 4 of the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions. They tell an Owner what requires the Architectural Review Committee's approval, how to apply, how long review takes, and what materials, colors and dimensions are acceptable, including the approved exterior color palette the Committee last updated January 15, 2024.

## Article 1 - When Approval Is Required

### 1.1 Changes Requiring Approval

Committee approval is required before an Owner makes any change to the exterior of a home or Lot, including a new exterior paint color, a new or replacement fence, a shed or other outbuilding, a deck or patio, a change of roofing material, replacement windows, a solar installation, a pool or hot tub, an outdoor EV charger, or any landscaping change beyond planting flowers, shrubs or replacing an existing bed. Repainting a home in the same previously approved color does not require a new application, since the color itself has already been reviewed.

## Article 2 - Application and Review Process

### 2.1 How to Apply

An Owner applies using the architectural request form available from management or the owner portal, describing the proposed change and attaching samples, photographs, a site sketch or manufacturer specifications as appropriate to the type of change. The Committee, chaired by Paul Wexler, meets the first Monday of each month at 6:30 pm in the clubhouse and reviews complete applications in the order received.

### 2.2 Review Timeline and Deemed Approval

The Committee must respond to a complete application within 30 days of its submission. If the Committee has not responded within 30 days, the application is deemed approved exactly as submitted, and the Owner may proceed. An incomplete application does not start the 30-day clock; management will notify the Owner of what is missing, and the clock begins once the complete package is received.

## Article 3 - Approved Exterior Colors

### 3.1 Body and Siding Colors

Sheppard Center's approved palette contains 14 colors across body, trim and accent categories, chosen to keep the neighborhood's tree-lined streets visually harmonious. The seven approved body and siding colors are Colonial White, Dove Gray, Sage, Wheat, Slate Blue, Clay Brown and Soft Taupe. Sage is a soft, muted green-gray, distinct from unapproved greener or yellower tones sometimes marketed under similar names; an Owner proposing a color they believe matches Sage should bring the actual paint chip or can label to the Committee rather than relying on a product name alone, since two colors can share a similar name while reading very differently on a wall or shed in daylight.

### 3.2 Trim and Accent Colors

The four approved trim colors are Bright White, Charcoal, Linen and Pewter, used for window trim, fascia and soffits. The three approved accent and door colors are Hunter Green, Brick Red and Navy, intended for front doors and shutters. An Owner may mix any approved body color with any approved trim and accent color; the Committee does not restrict combinations within the palette, only colors drawn from outside it.

### 3.3 Colors Outside the Palette

A color not on the list in Sections 3.1 and 3.2, however close it may appear to an approved color on a paint chip, a screen or in a store display, is not approved and requires a variance application reviewed individually by the Committee, which may request a sample painted on an inconspicuous section of the structure before deciding. A commonly confused example is a brighter, yellower green sometimes sold as "Meadow Green" or similar names; it is not Sage and is not on the approved palette, even though the names and chips can look alike side by side. The burden is on the Owner to show that an already-applied color matches an approved entry, not merely that it is similar.

## Article 4 - Fences

### 4.1 Fence Height and Materials

A fence in a back yard may be no taller than 6 feet; a fence in a front yard may be no taller than 4 feet. Approved materials are white vinyl and natural cedar; chain link is not permitted anywhere on a Lot. A new fence, or the replacement of an existing fence in a different material or color, requires Committee approval before installation, with a response due within 30 days as described in Article 2.

## Article 5 - Sheds and Outbuildings

### 5.1 Shed Size, Placement and Color

A Lot may have one shed, no larger than 120 square feet and no taller than 10 feet, placed behind the home so it is not visible from the street where possible. A shed must be painted to match the home's existing approved body color -- for a home already painted Sage, Dove Gray or any other approved body color from Section 3.1, the shed must match that same color, not merely something close to it. Painting a shed in any other color, including one not on the approved palette, is a violation requiring either a corrective repaint to a matching approved color or an approved variance under Section 3.3. Like any other exterior change, a shed requires Committee approval before it is built.

## Article 6 - Solar Energy Systems

### 6.1 Solar Panel Review

Solar panels are allowed under state law, which protects an Owner's right to install them, and the Committee cannot deny a solar application on the basis of appearance alone. An Owner must still submit an application so the Committee can note the proposed placement, panel layout and any visible mounting hardware, and may suggest a less visible placement on the same roof plane where one is equally effective, but may not refuse the installation outright.

## Article 7 - Satellite Dishes and Antennas

### 7.1 Dish Placement

A satellite dish under one meter in diameter is permitted wherever needed for reception. Where reception allows a choice of location, the Owner should favor a placement that does not face the street, but this preference does not override the practical need for a working signal, and the Committee will approve a street-facing placement if it is demonstrated to be necessary.

## Article 8 - Pools, Hot Tubs and Decks

### 8.1 Private Pools, Hot Tubs and Decking

An in-ground or substantial above-ground pool or hot tub on a Lot requires Committee approval of its design and placement, and must be enclosed by a fence that meets the City of Maple Grove's pool safety code, independent of and in addition to the Association's own fence height and material rules in Article 4. A deck or patio built in connection with a pool, or on its own, also requires approval under Article 1.

## Article 9 - Landscaping and Tree Removal

### 9.1 Landscaping Standards and Fast-Track Review

Lawns must be kept under 6 inches in height and planting beds kept weeded. Removing a tree with a trunk over 6 inches in diameter requires Committee approval, to prevent the loss of the mature tree canopy that lines Sheppard Center's streets. Drought-tolerant landscaping replacing traditional turf is encouraged and receives fast-track review, typically completed well inside the standard 30-day window, in recognition of its water-saving benefit to the whole community.

## Article 10 - EV Charging Equipment

### 10.1 EV Chargers

A home EV charger installed entirely inside the garage requires no Committee approval, since it is not visible from outside the home. An EV charger mounted outside, such as on an exterior wall or a freestanding post in the driveway, requires approval of its placement to keep cabling and hardware from becoming visually intrusive to neighboring Lots.

## Article 11 - Denials, Variances and Appeals

### 11.1 Denials and the Appeal Process

If the Committee denies an application, it provides the Owner a written reason, which may include guidance on a resubmission that would be approvable, such as choosing a palette color or a compliant fence material. An Owner may appeal a denial to the Board, which may affirm, reverse, or send the matter back to the Committee with instructions, at the Board's next regular meeting following the appeal request.

## Article 12 - Records and Inspection

### 12.1 Application Records and Construction Inspection

The Committee keeps a file of every application it receives, including the date filed, the date it was deemed complete, its decision and any conditions attached, such as the gate-orientation condition the Committee has attached to fence approvals in the past. These records are available to the Owner who filed the application and to the Board, and help the Committee apply past decisions consistently when a similar request comes in later. Once a project is underway, management or a Committee member may informally observe work visible from the street, such as a fence or shed under construction, to confirm it matches what was approved; an Owner who changes the approved plan mid-project, such as switching from the approved cedar to an unapproved material, should notify the Committee immediately rather than waiting for the change to be noticed, since an unannounced deviation is treated as an unapproved architectural change under the Fine and Enforcement Policy.
