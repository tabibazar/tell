# Fine and Enforcement Policy for Sheppard Center
<!-- type: enforcement; adopted: 2010-01-01; amended: 2021-09-08 -->

This Fine and Enforcement Policy sets out how the Sheppard Center Homeowners Association enforces the Declaration, the Bylaws, the Rules and Regulations and the Architectural Guidelines: how a violation is noticed, how long an Owner has to cure it, what hearing rights apply, the fine schedule, and how an Owner may appeal.

## Part 1 - Purpose and Authority

### 1.1 Purpose and Authority

The Board adopts this policy under the enforcement authority given to it by Article 9 of the Declaration. Its purpose is to apply fines consistently and fairly across all 212 homes, to give every Owner a genuine opportunity to cure a violation before a fine is imposed, and to provide a hearing before any serious or continuing fine takes effect. Management, currently Lakeside Property Management under community manager Dana Ortiz, administers the day-to-day notice process on the Board's behalf.

## Part 2 - Notice and Cure Process

### 2.1 Courtesy Notice

Most violations begin with a courtesy notice: a first written notice carrying no fine and giving the Owner 14 days to cure the condition. A courtesy notice is sent for a first-time issue such as a trash bin left visible past the pickup window, an overgrown lawn, or a pet off leash. The notice describes the violation, cites the rule involved, and explains what correcting it requires.

### 2.2 Second Notice

If the same violation is not cured within the 14-day period, or recurs afterward, the Association issues a second notice carrying a $50 fine and a further 14 days to cure. The second notice also advises the Owner that continuing or repeated noncompliance may be referred to the Board for a hearing, at which a larger or daily fine could apply.

## Part 3 - Hearings

### 3.1 Right to a Hearing

Before a continuing daily fine accrues, or before certain fines described in Part 4 are finalized, the Owner is entitled to a hearing before the Board. Management schedules the hearing for an upcoming Board meeting and gives the Owner written notice of the date, time and the violation at issue. The Owner may attend the hearing in person or by video, present evidence, bring documents or photographs, and explain the circumstances before the Board decides.

### 3.2 Conduct of the Hearing

A hearing is conducted as part of the Board's regular meeting agenda, separate from the general homeowner open forum. The Board hears from management or the committee that raised the violation, then from the Owner, and may ask questions of either. The Board then votes on whether the violation is sustained and, if so, what fine or corrective action applies, recording its decision in the meeting minutes.

## Part 4 - Fine Schedule

### 4.1 General Fine Schedule

The table below sets out the Association's standard fines.

| Violation | Fine | Cure Period |
|---|---|---|
| Courtesy notice (first time, most rules) | $0 | 14 days |
| Second notice, same issue | $50 | 14 more days |
| Continuing violation after a hearing | $25 per day, capped at $1,000 | None |
| Unapproved architectural change | $250 plus restoring it | 30 days |
| Short-term rental | $500 per stay | Immediately |
| Pool or clubhouse rule broken | $100, and possible 30-day loss of access | None |

### 4.2 Unapproved Architectural Changes

An exterior change made without the Committee's required approval, such as an unapproved fence, shed color, or structure, carries a $250 fine in addition to the Owner's obligation to restore the change to its prior, approved condition, or to obtain a variance if one is available under the Architectural Guidelines. The Owner has 30 days from the date of the notice to either restore the condition or secure approval; if the Owner instead applies for approval of the existing change within that period, the $250 fine may be held pending the outcome of that application at the Board's discretion.

### 4.3 Short-Term Rentals and Amenity Violations

A short-term rental booking of fewer than 30 days results in a $500 fine per stay, assessed against the Owner regardless of whether the Owner or a booking platform collected the rent, and the rental must stop immediately upon notice. A pool or clubhouse rule violation, such as glass at the pool or smoking indoors, carries a $100 fine and may result in a 30-day suspension of that Owner's amenity access, including their pool fob, at the Board's discretion based on the severity of the conduct.

## Part 5 - Continuing Violations

### 5.1 Daily Fines After a Hearing

If the Board sustains a violation at a hearing and the Owner does not cure it afterward, the Association may impose a fine of $25 per day until the violation is cured, up to a maximum of $1,000 for that violation. The daily fine stops accruing once the Owner cures the condition or the Board otherwise resolves the matter, and any amount already accrued remains due and is collected like any other assessment.

## Part 6 - Appeals

### 6.1 Appeal of a Fine

An Owner who disputes a notice, a fine or a hearing decision may request reconsideration by submitting a written appeal to management within 14 days of the decision, explaining the basis for the appeal, such as new evidence or a claim that the condition complies with the governing documents, for example that an exterior color matches an approved palette entry. The Board considers the appeal at its next regular meeting and may uphold, reduce, or dismiss the fine. An Owner may attend that meeting to speak to the appeal under the same hearing rights described in Part 3.

## Part 7 - Payment and Records

### 7.1 Payment of Fines and Record-Keeping

A fine becomes due 30 days after it is finalized, whether by the expiration of an uncontested cure period or by a Board decision after a hearing or appeal. An unpaid fine is added to the Owner's account and is subject to the same late fee, interest and collection procedure as unpaid assessments under the Collection Policy. The Association keeps a record of every notice, fine and hearing outcome in the Owner's file, available to that Owner on request to management.

## Part 8 - Notice Delivery and Multiple Violations

### 8.1 How Notices Are Delivered

A courtesy notice, second notice or hearing notice is sent to the Owner's address of record and, where the Owner has provided one, to their email on file, and is also visible to the Owner on the Lakeside owner portal under that Lot's account. An Owner who rents their home remains the one who receives notice under this policy, since the Owner, not the tenant, is responsible for compliance with the governing documents, though management will copy a registered tenant on a notice involving the tenant's own conduct where a current tenant contact is on file.

### 8.2 Multiple or Unrelated Violations

Separate violations, even at the same Lot, are tracked and noticed independently; a second notice for a trash-bin violation does not affect the cure period running on an unrelated shed-color violation at the same home, and each follows its own notice, cure and fine schedule under Part 2 and Part 4. Where an Owner has several open violations at once, management may, at the Board's direction, combine them into a single hearing for efficiency, but each violation is still evaluated and decided on its own facts.
